Applying the dual rules to LP1 gives one dual variable per primal edge-constraint and one dual constraint per primal vertex-variable. Introduce \(y_{uv}\ge 0\) for each edge constraint \(x_u+x_v\ge 1\). The primal right-hand sides are all \(1\), so the dual objective is \(\max\sum_{uv\in E} y_{uv}\). Each primal variable \(x_v\) has objective coefficient \(1\) and appears in the constraints of exactly the edges incident to \(v\), giving the dual constraint \(\sum_{u\in N(v)} y_{uv}\le 1\). So DLP1 is \[ \max \sum_{uv\in E} y_{uv} \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \sum_{u\in N(v)} y_{uv}\le 1 \ \ \forall v\in V,\qquad y_{uv}\ge 0 \ \ \forall uv\in E. \]

This puts nonnegative weights on the edges so that the total weight at each vertex is at most \(1\), and maximizes the total edge weight — a fractional matching.

Intuition. Dualizing is a fixed recipe: edges become variables and vertices become "total incident weight at most \(1\)" constraints, giving a fractional matching.
