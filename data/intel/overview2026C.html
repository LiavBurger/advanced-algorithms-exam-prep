How he builds a paper

  • True/false is a lecture quiz. 39 of 40 past T/F items restate a slide claim with a twist: negate it, swap a constant, drop a hypothesis, or overgeneralise. A few are textbook exercises (Motwani–Raghavan Problem 12.22, Bertsimas–Tsitsiklis 3.6(a)). 29 of the 40 answers were True, and slot (a) was an LP fact in 7 of 10 papers.
  • Written problems in 2026 were fresh textbook exercises lifted nearly verbatim (Williamson–Shmoys Ch. 5–6, Erickson FFT, Jukna, DPV) plus re-skins of his own recent problems.
  • Moed C (2024C, 2025C): about two-thirds of the points re-skinned same-year A/B problems or older templates. Every Moed C had one "re-prove a class theorem with a changed parameter" problem. Each added one or two topics that its year's A/B skipped, and its T/F items reused sub-topics from the previous sitting.

How to use this tab

Do the mock as a timed paper (it is the single most likely problem per slot). Then do the alternates: together with the mock they cover the families that carry most of the probability. Use the T/F pool for fast drills; each item cites the slide or exercise it is built from. The Why predicted box under each problem gives the evidence and a calibrated probability. Treat those numbers as honest odds, not guarantees.
