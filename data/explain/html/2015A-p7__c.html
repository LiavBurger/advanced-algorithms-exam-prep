Claim The dual optimum is at least \(40\).

Strategy Exhibit one feasible primal solution of value \(40\) and invoke strong duality.

Key idea Any feasible primal value is a lower bound on the primal optimum, which equals the dual optimum.

Why this move You never need to solve the dual — a single feasible primal point plus the duality theorem pins down the bound for free.

  1. Reuse part (a). After the pivot, set the non-basic variables to \(0\): \(x_1=x_3=x_4=0,\ s_3=0\), giving \(x_2=10,\ s_1=2,\ s_2=37\).
  2. Check feasibility and value. All variables are non-negative, so every primal constraint holds; the objective is \(2x_1+4x_2+3x_3+x_4=4\cdot10=40\).
  3. Lower-bound the primal. Since the primal is a maximization with a feasible point of value \(40\), its optimum is at least \(40\).
  4. Cross to the dual. By strong duality the dual optimum equals the primal optimum, so the dual optimum is also at least \(40\).
