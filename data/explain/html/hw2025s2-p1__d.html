Here \(A\) is the normalized adjacency matrix of the \(d\)-regular graph and \(B=\tfrac12(I+A)\) is the lazy walk matrix; \(x\) is the unit eigenvector of its second eigenvalue \(\lambda_2\), so \(Bx=\lambda_2 x\). We rewrite \(\lambda_2\) as a quadratic form and lower-bound the "defect" from \(1\).

  1. Turn \(\lambda_2\) into a quadratic form. Using \(\langle x,x\rangle=\|x\|_2^2=1\) and \(Bx=\lambda_2x\), \[\lambda_2=\lambda_2\langle x,x\rangle=\langle \lambda_2 x,x\rangle=\langle Bx,x\rangle=x^TBx=\sum_{k,\ell}B_{k,\ell}\,x_kx_\ell.\]
  2. Symmetrize each product. Apply the identity \(x_kx_\ell=\tfrac12\big(x_k^2+x_\ell^2-(x_k-x_\ell)^2\big)\): \[\lambda_2=\tfrac12\sum_{k,\ell}B_{k,\ell}\,x_k^2+\tfrac12\sum_{k,\ell}B_{k,\ell}\,x_\ell^2-\tfrac12\sum_{k,\ell}B_{k,\ell}(x_k-x_\ell)^2.\]
  3. Use that \(B\) is doubly stochastic. Each row of \(B\) sums to \(1\), so \(\sum_{k,\ell}B_{k,\ell}x_k^2=\sum_k x_k^2\sum_\ell B_{k,\ell}=\sum_k x_k^2=1\); by symmetry the \(x_\ell^2\) sum equals \(1\) as well. The first two sums contribute \(\tfrac12(1+1)=1\), giving \[\lambda_2=1-\tfrac12\sum_{k,\ell}B_{k,\ell}(x_k-x_\ell)^2.\]
  4. Lower-bound the remaining sum. Every term \(B_{k,\ell}(x_k-x_\ell)^2\ge 0\), so we may keep only the edge \((u,v)\) from part (c). There \(|x_u-x_v|\ge \frac{1}{n\sqrt n}\) gives \((x_u-x_v)^2\ge \frac{1}{n^3}\), and since \((u,v)\) is an edge, \(A_{u,v}\ge \frac1d\), hence \(B_{u,v}=\tfrac12 A_{u,v}\ge \frac{1}{2d}\). Therefore \(\tfrac12\sum_{k,\ell}B_{k,\ell}(x_k-x_\ell)^2\ge \tfrac12\cdot\frac{1}{2d}\cdot\frac{1}{n^3}=\frac{1}{4dn^3}\).
Combining, \(\lambda_2\le 1-\dfrac{1}{4dn^3}\); equivalently the spectral gap satisfies \(1-\lambda_2\ge \dfrac{1}{4dn^3}\).
