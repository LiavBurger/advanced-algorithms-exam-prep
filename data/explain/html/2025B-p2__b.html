We relax the ILP from (a) and round at the threshold \(1/f\):

  1. Replace \(x_i\in\{0,1\}\) by \(0\le x_i\le 1\) and solve the LP for an optimal \(x^*\). Since every integer cover is LP-feasible, \(\text{LPOPT}=\sum_i c_i x_i^*\le\text{OPT}\).
  2. Round: include \(S_i\) in the cover exactly when \(x_i^*\ge 1/f\); call the resulting vector \(x\).

The output is a valid cover. Fix an element \(j\). It appears in at most \(f\) sets, and the LP constraint gives \(\sum_{i:S_i\ni j}x_i^*\ge 1\). A sum of at most \(f\) nonnegative terms reaching \(1\) must have some term at least \(1/f\), so some set containing \(j\) has \(x_i^*\ge 1/f\) and is taken; thus \(j\) is covered.

The weight is at most \(f\cdot\text{OPT}\). For every \(i\) we have \(x_i\le f\,x_i^*\): if \(x_i=0\) this holds since \(x_i^*\ge 0\), and if \(x_i=1\) then \(x_i^*\ge 1/f\) makes \(f\,x_i^*\ge 1\). Summing with weights \(c_i\ge 0\),

\[\text{ALG}=\sum_i c_i x_i\le f\sum_i c_i x_i^*=f\cdot\text{LPOPT}\le f\cdot\text{OPT}.\]

The LP is solvable in polynomial time, so this is a polynomial-time \(f\)-approximation.

Intuition. With only \(f\) sets over an element, one must carry a \(1/f\) share, so rounding at \(1/f\) still covers it while losing at most a factor of \(f\).
