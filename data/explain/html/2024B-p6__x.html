Use the following randomized algorithm: for every pair \(i\in L,\ j\in R\), independently include the edge \((i,j)\) with probability \(\tfrac12\) (and leave it out otherwise). This runs in time polynomial in \(n\): there are \(n^2\) pairs and we flip one coin per pair. We claim that, for a suitable constant \(C\), with probability at least \(0.9\) the resulting \(G\) is \((C\log n,\tfrac1{100})\)-balanced.

Fix a size \(m\ge k\) and two subsets \(S\subseteq L,\ T\subseteq R\) with \(|S|=|T|=m\). For \(i\in S,\ j\in T\), let \(X_{ij}\) be an indicator random variable for the event that \((i,j)\) is an edge, so \(\mathbb{E}[X_{ij}]=\tfrac12\). Then \(|E(S,T)|=\sum_{i\in S,\,j\in T}X_{ij}\), and by linearity of expectation

\[\mathbb{E}\big[|E(S,T)|\big]=\sum_{i\in S,\,j\in T}\tfrac12=\tfrac12 m^2.\]

The \(X_{ij}\) are independent, and the deviation \(\tfrac1{100}m^2\) is a constant fraction of a sum of \(m^2\) independent bits, so by the Chernoff bound the chance of deviating that much from the mean shrinks fast:

\[\Pr\!\Big[\,\big||E(S,T)|-\tfrac12 m^2\big|\ge \tfrac1{100}m^2\,\Big]\le e^{-c\,m^2}\]

for some absolute constant \(c>0\).

Now take a union bound over the choices of \(S\) and \(T\). The number of ways to pick \(S\) and \(T\) of size \(m\) is \(\binom{n}{m}^2\le n^{2m}\), so

\[\Pr[\exists\,S,T\text{ of size }m\text{ with a bad edge count}]\le n^{2m}\,e^{-c m^2}=e^{\,2m\ln n-c m^2}.\]

Write the exponent as \(2m\ln n-cm^2=m(2\ln n-cm)\). For \(m\ge k=C\log n\) with the constant \(C\) chosen large enough, \(cm\) beats \(2\ln n\) by enough that this bound is at most \(\tfrac1{10n}\); and since the exponent only becomes more negative as \(m\) grows, the bound \(\le\tfrac1{10n}\) holds for every \(m\) in the range \(k\le m\le n\).

Finally union bound over these sizes (there are at most \(n\) of them):

\[\Pr\big[G\text{ is not }(C\log n,\tfrac1{100})\text{-balanced}\big]\le n\cdot\tfrac1{10n}=\tfrac1{10}.\]

Hence with probability at least \(1-\tfrac1{10}=0.9\) the graph \(G\) is \((C\log n,\tfrac1{100})\)-balanced, as required.

Intuition. Random edges make every pair of equal-sized sides carry about half its pairs as edges, and Chernoff makes each failure chance tiny enough to survive counting all the subsets at once.
