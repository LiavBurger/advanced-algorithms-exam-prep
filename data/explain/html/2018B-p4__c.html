The pivot is chosen uniformly at random, so for each \(k\) from \(1\) to \(n\), the algorithm picks the \(k\)-th largest element as the pivot with probability \(1/n\). After partitioning (which costs \(\Theta(n)\)), it recurses on one side; in the worst case it recurses on the larger of the two parts, which when the pivot is the \(k\)-th largest has size \(\max(k-1,\,n-k)\). Conditioning on the pivot and taking expectations, \[E[T(n)]\le \frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\max\big(E[T(k-1)],\,E[T(n-k)]\big)+\Theta(n).\] As \(k\) runs over \(1,\dots,n\), the value \(\max(k-1,\,n-k)\) always lies between \(n/2\) and \(n-1\), and each such index appears at most twice in the sum. Hence we can bound the sum by \(2\) times the terms with index from \(n/2\) to \(n-1\): \[E[T(n)]\le \frac{2}{n}\sum_{k=n/2}^{n-1}E[T(k)]+\Theta(n),\] which is the claimed recurrence.

Intuition. Every pivot leaves a "larger half" of size between \(n/2\) and \(n\), and each such size is hit at most twice, giving the factor \(2/n\).
