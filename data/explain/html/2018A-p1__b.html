The recurrence is \(T(n)=T\!\big(\tfrac{5n}{6}\big)+T\!\big(\tfrac{n}{10}\big)+\Theta(n^2)\): the \(\Theta(n^2)\) split cost dominates the \(\Theta(n)\) combine cost, so the local work at a node of size \(n\) is \(\Theta(n^2)\). This is not a Master-Theorem form (unequal subproblem sizes), so reason level by level via the recursion tree.

Key idea: the total subproblem size shrinks by the constant factor \(\tfrac{5}{6}+\tfrac{1}{10}=\tfrac{28}{30}<1\) each level, and because work is superlinear (\(n^2\)) the levels form a decreasing geometric series dominated by the top.
  1. Level work. The root does \(\Theta(n^2)\). Its children have sizes \(\tfrac{5n}{6}\) and \(\tfrac{n}{10}\); their combined work is \(\Theta\!\big((\tfrac{5n}{6})^2+(\tfrac{n}{10})^2\big)=\big(\tfrac{25}{36}+\tfrac1{100}\big)\Theta(n^2)\approx 0.70\,\Theta(n^2)\).
  2. Geometric decay. Squaring makes each level a constant factor \(r=\tfrac{25}{36}+\tfrac1{100}<1\) of the level above, so total work \(=\Theta(n^2)\,(1+r+r^2+\cdots)=\Theta(n^2)\cdot\tfrac{1}{1-r}=\Theta(n^2)\). (Depth is \(O(\log n)\) since sizes shrink by a constant factor, but the sum converges regardless.)
  3. Lower bound. The root alone costs \(\Theta(n^2)\), so \(T(n)=\Omega(n^2)\). With the matching upper bound, \(T(n)=\Theta(n^2)\).
