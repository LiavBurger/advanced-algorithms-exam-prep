Weak duality theorem. Let \((P)\) be an LP in standard form, \(\min c^\top x\) s.t. \(Ax=b,\ x\ge 0\), and let \((D)\) be its dual, \(\max b^\top y\) s.t. \(A^\top y\le c\). Then for every feasible \(x\) of \((P)\) and every feasible \(y\) of \((D)\) we have \(c^\top x\ge b^\top y\).

Proof. Let \(x\) and \(y\) be feasible for \((P)\) and \((D)\) respectively. Since \(A^\top y\le c\) coordinatewise and \(x\ge 0\), multiplying the inequality on the left by \(x^\top\) preserves its direction, giving

\[x^\top A^\top y\le x^\top c,\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad y^\top A x\le c^\top x.\]

Now use primal feasibility \(Ax=b\) on the left side: \(y^\top A x=y^\top b\), so \(y^\top b\le c^\top x\). Since these are scalars, \(y^\top b=b^\top y\), and therefore \(b^\top y\le c^\top x\), which is the claim. \(\qquad\square\)

Intuition. Nonnegativity of \(x\) lets the dual inequality survive being multiplied through, and the primal equality collapses the left side to \(b^\top y\), so every dual value sits below every primal value.
