Claim the basis \(\{x_1,s_1\}\) gives the vertex \(x_1=3,\ x_2=0,\ x_3=0,\ s_1=11,\ s_2=0\).

Strategy a basis names which variables may be nonzero; set the others to \(0\) and solve the two equality constraints for the basic ones.

Key idea the nonbasic variables \(x_2,x_3,s_2\) are all \(0\), which collapses each constraint to a single unknown.

Why this move a vertex of the feasible region is exactly a basic solution, so "read off the vertex" just means zero the nonbasic variables and back out the rest.

  1. Identify nonbasic. Of \(x_1,x_2,x_3,s_1,s_2\), the basis is \(\{x_1,s_1\}\), so \(x_2=x_3=s_2=0\).
  2. Solve for \(x_1\). From \(3x_1-x_2+2x_3+s_2=9\) with the zeros, \(3x_1=9\), so \(x_1=3\).
  3. Solve for \(s_1\). From \(2x_1-x_2-s_1=-5\) with \(x_2=0\): \(6-s_1=-5\), so \(s_1=11\).

Both basic values came out nonnegative (\(x_1=3\), \(s_1=11\)), so the vertex is feasible — the basis \(\{x_1,s_1\}\) sits at a real corner of the region.
