Claim True — at most half the students score at least twice the average.

Strategy Model a random student's grade and apply Markov's inequality.

Key idea Grades are non-negative, so the fraction scoring at least twice the mean is bounded by Markov.

Why this move "Fraction of students above a multiple of the average" is exactly a tail probability of a non-negative variable, which is Markov's home ground.

  1. Set up the variable. Let \(X\) be the grade of a student chosen uniformly at random. The average grade is then \(\mathbb{E}[X]\), and for any threshold \(a\), \(\Pr[X\ge a]\) is the fraction of students scoring at least \(a\).
  2. Apply Markov. Grades are non-negative, so \(\Pr[X \ge 2\,\mathbb{E}[X]] \le \tfrac{\mathbb{E}[X]}{2\,\mathbb{E}[X]} = \tfrac12\).
  3. Read it back. That probability is just the fraction of students scoring at least twice the average, so at most half of them do.
