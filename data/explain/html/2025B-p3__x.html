Let \(x\) be a basic feasible solution of a standard-form LP (minimize \(c^Tx\) subject to \(Ax=b,\ x\ge 0\)) with basis \(B\); split the columns of \(A\) into the basic part \(A_B\) and the nonbasic part \(A_N\), and correspondingly \(x=(x_B,x_N)\) and \(c=(c_B,c_N)\). We show that if the reduced cost vector \(\tilde c_N\ge 0\), then \(x\) is optimal. The idea is to rewrite the objective so its dependence on the (free) nonbasic variables is exactly \(\tilde c_N\).

  1. Express basic variables. The equality constraint is \(A_Bx_B+A_Nx_N=b\). Since \(A_B\) is invertible (it is a basis), \(x_B=A_B^{-1}b-A_B^{-1}A_Nx_N\). This holds for every feasible point, not just \(x\).
  2. Substitute into the objective. Then \[c^Tx=c_B^Tx_B+c_N^Tx_N=c_B^T\big(A_B^{-1}b-A_B^{-1}A_Nx_N\big)+c_N^Tx_N=c_B^TA_B^{-1}b+\big(c_N^T-c_B^TA_B^{-1}A_N\big)x_N.\] The bracketed row vector is precisely the reduced cost \(\tilde c_N^T\), so \(c^Tx=c_B^TA_B^{-1}b+\tilde c_N^Tx_N\).
  3. Value at \(x\). At the basic feasible solution \(x\) the nonbasic variables vanish, \(x_N=0\), so \(c^Tx=c_B^TA_B^{-1}b\).
  4. Lower bound for any feasible \(y\). The identity in step 2 applies to any feasible \(y\ge 0\), giving \(c^Ty=c_B^TA_B^{-1}b+\tilde c_N^Ty_N\). Because \(\tilde c_N\ge 0\) and \(y_N\ge 0\), the term \(\tilde c_N^Ty_N\ge 0\), so \(c^Ty\ge c_B^TA_B^{-1}b=c^Tx\).
  5. Thus \(x\) attains the smallest objective value among all feasible points, i.e. \(x\) is optimal.
