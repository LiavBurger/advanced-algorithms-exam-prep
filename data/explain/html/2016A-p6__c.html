Claim the dual from part (b) is (fractional) weighted vertex cover.

Strategy read each variable as an amount of "resource" placed on a vertex and read each constraint as a demand on an edge.

Key idea the constraint \(y_u+y_v\ge w(uv)\) says every edge's demand \(w(uv)\) is met by the resource sitting on its two endpoints.

Why this move "sum over the two endpoints \(\ge\) something, minimize total" is the signature of a covering problem, so naming it as vertex cover is immediate.

  1. Variables as weights. Each \(y_v\ge 0\) is how much weight (budget) we put on vertex \(v\).
  2. Constraints as coverage. For every edge \(uv\), the weights on its endpoints must total at least \(w(uv)\).
  3. Objective as thrift. Minimize the total weight placed, \(\min\sum_v y_v\).

So it is: put enough weight on the vertices that each edge \(e\) has at least \(w(e)\) on its endpoints, using as little total weight as possible — fractional because we are in the LP, not the integer program.
