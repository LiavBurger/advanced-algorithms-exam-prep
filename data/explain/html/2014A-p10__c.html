To decide which variable should enter the basis, simplex rewrites the objective so that it only mentions the current non-basic variables \(x_2,x_3,s_1\) (which are all at \(0\)). The sign of each coefficient then tells us whether nudging that variable up from \(0\) would raise or lower the objective.

  1. Take the objective \(2x_1+5x_2+2x_3\) and replace the basic variable \(x_1\) by its dictionary expression \(x_1=10-2x_2-s_1\) from part (b): \[ 2x_1+5x_2+2x_3=2(10-2x_2-s_1)+5x_2+2x_3 . \]
  2. Expand and collect like terms: \[ =20-4x_2-2s_1+5x_2+2x_3=20+x_2+2x_3-2s_1 . \]
  3. Read off the coefficients of the non-basic variables: \(x_2\) has \(+1\), \(x_3\) has \(+2\), and \(s_1\) has \(-2\). Since \(x_2\) and \(x_3\) have positive coefficients, increasing either of them (from its current value \(0\)) increases the objective; \(s_1\) would decrease it, so it is not a candidate.
  4. Among the improving variables we pick the one with the largest coefficient, i.e. the steepest per-unit gain. That is \(x_3\) with rate \(+2\) versus \(+1\) for \(x_2\).
The entering variable is \(x_3\): it offers the best rate of increase of the objective, \(+2\) per unit.

This is the standard (Dantzig) pivoting rule — enter the non-basic variable with the most positive reduced cost — and it sets up the ratio test in part (d) that determines who must leave.
