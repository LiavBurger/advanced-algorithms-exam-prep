Claim the general dual of the semi-matching BIP is weighted vertex cover with \(V_1\)-vertices costing \(1\) and \(V_2\)-vertices costing \(2\).

Strategy read the dual variable per vertex as "is this vertex chosen?", the constraint per edge as "cover this edge", and the objective coefficients as the caps carried over from the primal.

Key idea the primal caps \(1\) and \(2\) become the dual costs, so the two sides of the bipartition get weights \(1\) and \(2\).

Why this move the dual has "one variable per vertex, one \(y_u+y_v\ge 1\) per edge, minimize total" — that shape is precisely vertex cover, so we just name it and attach the weights.

  1. Variables. One \(y_v\in\{0,1\}\) per vertex, meaning \(v\) is put in the cover.
  2. Constraints. Edge \((u,v)\) contributes \(y_u+y_v\ge 1\): at least one endpoint of every edge is chosen — the cover condition.
  3. Objective. Costs are the primal caps: \(1\) for \(V_1\), \(2\) for \(V_2\), so \(\min\sum_{v\in V_1}y_v+2\sum_{v\in V_2}y_v\).

So the dual asks for the cheapest set of vertices touching every edge, charging \(1\) per \(V_1\)-vertex and \(2\) per \(V_2\)-vertex — weighted vertex cover.
