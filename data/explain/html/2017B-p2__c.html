Goal Write the LP relaxation of the knapsack BIP from part (b).

Strategy Keep the objective and capacity row, and just drop integrality — replace \(x_i\in\{0,1\}\) with the interval \(0\le x_i\le1\).

Key idea \(\{0,1\}\) is the integer endpoints of \([0,1]\); allowing everything in between makes the program linear and polynomial-time solvable.

Why this move Relaxing integrality is the standard way to get a tractable LP whose optimum upper-bounds the hard integer optimum.

  1. Relax the integrality. Swap \(x_i\in\{0,1\}\) for the two linear inequalities \(x_i\le1\) and \(x_i\ge0\), letting each \(x_i\) be any real in \([0,1]\).
  2. Carry the rest over. The objective \(\sum_i b_i x_i\) and constraint \(\sum_i w_i x_i\le W\) are already linear, so they stay as is: \[\max \sum_i b_i x_i \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \sum_i w_i x_i \le W,\qquad x_i \le 1,\ x_i \ge 0\ \forall i.\]
  3. Note what it buys. This LP solves in polynomial time and its optimum is at least the integer optimum (every 0/1 solution is still feasible here). Reading \(x_i\in[0,1]\) as "the fraction of item \(i\) taken" makes it the fractional knapsack.
