Goal Prove \(\mathbb{E}[T(n)] \le \frac{2}{n}\sum_{k=n/2}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}[T(k)] + \Theta(n)\).

Strategy Condition on the pivot's rank, pessimistically charge each case to its larger side, then average over the uniform pivot.

Key idea Selection recurses on only one side, but bounding by the bigger side and counting how often each big size occurs collapses the sum to the upper half with a factor of \(2\).

Why this move We don't know which side the recursion takes, so the safe upper bound is "the worse of the two," and conditioning on the pivot is the clean way to enumerate the cases.

  1. Condition on the pivot. The pivot is uniform, so it is the \(k\)-th largest with probability \(\tfrac1n\) for each \(k\in\{1,\dots,n\}\). Partitioning always costs \(\Theta(n)\).
  2. Charge the larger side. If the pivot is the \(k\)-th largest, the two parts have sizes \(k-1\) and \(n-k\); since we recurse on one but can't say which, upper-bound it by \(\max\big(\mathbb{E}[T(k-1)],\mathbb{E}[T(n-k)]\big)\).
  3. Average over the pivot. By total expectation, \[\mathbb{E}[T(n)] \le \frac1n\sum_{k=1}^{n}\max\big(\mathbb{E}[T(k-1)],\mathbb{E}[T(n-k)]\big) + \Theta(n).\]
  4. Collapse the max. As \(k\) runs \(1,\dots,n\), the two sizes \(k-1\) and \(n-k\) each sweep \(0,\dots,n-1\), and the larger of the pair is always some index in the top half \(\{n/2,\dots,n-1\}\). Each such index is hit by at most two values of \(k\), so the sum of maxima is at most \(2\sum_{k=n/2}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}[T(k)]\).
  5. Finish. Substituting gives \(\mathbb{E}[T(n)] \le \frac{2}{n}\sum_{k=n/2}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}[T(k)] + \Theta(n)\), exactly the recurrence (rounding of \(n/2\) ignored as allowed).
