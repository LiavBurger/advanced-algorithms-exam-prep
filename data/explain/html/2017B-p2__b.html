We write knapsack as a binary integer linear program (BIP). Recall the input: items \(i\) with cost \(b_i\), weight \(w_i\), and a capacity \(W\); we want a subset of maximum total cost whose total weight is \(\le W\).

  1. Decision variables. Introduce one variable \(x_i\) per item, forced to be integral in \(\{0,1\}\): \(x_i=1\) means "item \(i\) is chosen," \(x_i=0\) means "not chosen." A 0/1 vector \(x\) is exactly a subset of items.
  2. Objective. The total cost of the chosen set is \(\sum_i b_i x_i\) (each chosen item contributes \(b_i\), each unchosen contributes \(0\)). We maximize it.
  3. Capacity constraint. The total weight is \(\sum_i w_i x_i\); it must not exceed \(W\), giving \(\sum_i w_i x_i \le W\).

Putting it together: \[\max \sum_i b_i x_i \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \sum_i w_i x_i \le W,\qquad x_i\in\{0,1\}\ \forall i.\]

The integrality constraint \(x_i\in\{0,1\}\) is what makes this an exact model of the combinatorial problem: it forbids taking a fraction of an item.
