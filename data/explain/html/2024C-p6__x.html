Claim if \((P)\) is infeasible and \((D)\) is feasible, then \((D)\) is unbounded.

Strategy use Farkas to extract an infeasibility direction, add multiples of it to the given dual point, and show the objective runs to \(+\infty\) while staying feasible.

Key idea Farkas turns "\(Ax=b,\ x\ge0\) has no solution" into a concrete vector \(y'\) that is a feasible direction pointing straight uphill for the dual.

Why this move infeasibility of a linear system is exactly what Farkas' Lemma converts into a usable certificate, which is the natural bridge from "no primal" to "unbounded dual".

  1. Get the certificate. Since \(Ax=b,\ x\ge0\) is infeasible, Farkas gives \(y'\) with \((y')^\top A\ge0\) and \((y')^\top b<0\).
  2. Move along it. Let \(y\) be the given feasible dual point and set \(y_\lambda=y-\lambda y'\) for \(\lambda>0\).
  3. Stay feasible. \(A^\top y_\lambda=A^\top y-\lambda A^\top y'\). Here \(A^\top y\le c\) (feasibility of \(y\)) and \(A^\top y'\ge0\) with \(\lambda>0\), so \(-\lambda A^\top y'\le0\); adding gives \(A^\top y_\lambda\le c\) for all \(\lambda>0\).
  4. Objective blows up. \(b^\top y_\lambda=b^\top y-\lambda\,b^\top y'\), and since \(b^\top y'=(y')^\top b<0\), the term \(-\lambda\,b^\top y'\) is positive and grows without bound as \(\lambda\to\infty\). So \(b^\top y_\lambda\to+\infty\) over feasible points, meaning \((D)\) is unbounded.
