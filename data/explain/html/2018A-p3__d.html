Goal Rewrite \(\min |2x-10|\) subject to \(4x-5\ge 0\) as a linear program.

Strategy Introduce an auxiliary variable \(z\) that upper-bounds the absolute value with two linear inequalities, then minimize \(z\).

Key idea \(|t|\) is the smallest \(z\) with \(z\ge t\) and \(z\ge -t\), so minimizing that \(z\) reproduces the absolute value.

Why this move An absolute value in the objective is the textbook trigger for the "\(z\) above both branches" trick — it is the standard way to linearize \(|\cdot|\) inside a minimization.

  1. Bracket the value. With \(t=2x-10\), add \(z\) and impose \(z\ge 2x-10\) and \(z\ge -2x+10\). Together they say \(z\ge |2x-10|\).
  2. Minimize \(z\). Setting the objective to \(\min z\) drives \(z\) down until one inequality is tight, forcing \(z = |2x-10|\). No sign constraint on \(z\) is needed — the two inequalities already give \(z\ge 0\).
  3. Keep the feasibility constraint. \(4x-5\ge 0\) is already linear, so it stays as is.

The LP is \[\min z \quad\text{s.t.}\quad 4x-5\ge 0,\qquad z\ge 2x-10,\qquad z\ge -2x+10.\] Everything is linear in \(x\) and \(z\), and the optimal \(z\) equals \(\min|2x-10|\) under the constraint.
