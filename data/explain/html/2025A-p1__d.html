Claim True — for a connected regular graph, the space \(\{v : Av = v\}\) has dimension exactly \(1\).

Strategy Identify that space as the eigenspace of the top eigenvalue \(1\) (in the normalized setting) and use connectivity to pin its multiplicity to one.

Key idea For a connected regular graph the largest eigenvalue is simple — the all-ones vector spans it and nothing else does.

Why this move "Dimension of an eigenspace" points straight to eigenvalue multiplicity, and connectivity is exactly the property that controls the gap below the top eigenvalue.

  1. What the space is. \(\{v : Av = v\}\) is the eigenspace for eigenvalue \(1\), so its dimension is the multiplicity of \(1\). (Here \(A\) is the normalized adjacency matrix, whose top eigenvalue is \(1\).)
  2. The top eigenvalue is attained. Each row sums to \(1\), so the all-ones vector satisfies \(A\mathbf{1} = \mathbf{1}\); thus \(1\) is an eigenvalue and the dimension is at least \(1\).
  3. Connectivity makes it simple. It was shown in the homework that for a connected graph the second eigenvalue is strictly below \(1\), so \(1\) has multiplicity exactly \(1\).

The eigenspace is just the line through the all-ones vector, so its dimension is one.
