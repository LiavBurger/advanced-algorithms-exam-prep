Goal Given \(f,g\) with \(f=g\cdot h\), recover the coefficients of \(h\) in time \(O(n\log n)\).

Strategy Move to point-value form at the \(n\)-th roots of unity: evaluate \(f\) and \(g\), divide pointwise, interpolate back.

Key idea Division of polynomials is trivial in point-value form — it's just \(f(\omega^i)/g(\omega^i)\) at each point — and the assumption \(g(\omega^i)\neq0\) makes every division safe.

Why this move Evaluating and interpolating at roots of unity is what the FFT does in \(O(n\log n)\), so pushing the hard operation into that domain is the natural reach.

  1. Evaluate. Compute \(f\) and \(g\) at \(1,\omega,\dots,\omega^{n-1}\). This is a DFT, done by the FFT in \(O(n\log n)\).
  2. Divide pointwise. For each \(i\) form \(f(\omega^i)/g(\omega^i)\) — \(n\) divisions, \(O(n)\) total, all well-defined since \(g(\omega^i)\neq0\).
  3. Interpolate. Because \(f=g\cdot h\), we have \(f(\omega^i)/g(\omega^i)=h(\omega^i)\). These are the values of \(h\) at the roots of unity, and interpolating them is an inverse DFT, again \(O(n\log n)\).
