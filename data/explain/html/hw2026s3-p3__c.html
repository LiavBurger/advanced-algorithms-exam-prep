Recall the threshold sets: for a level \(0\le r\le 1\), \(S(r)=\{v\in V:y_v\ge r\}\) collects the vertices whose \(y\)-value clears \(r\), and \(E(r)=\{(u,v)\in E:x_{uv}\ge r\}\) the edges whose \(x\)-value clears \(r\). Assuming the form \(x_{uv}=\min\{y_u,y_v\}\) justified in (b), we show an edge is "on" exactly when both its endpoints are "on."

Fix an edge \((u,v)\in E\) and a level \(r\). Then

\[ u,v\in S(r)\iff y_u\ge r\ \text{and}\ y_v\ge r\iff \min\{y_u,y_v\}\ge r\iff x_{uv}\ge r\iff (u,v)\in E(r). \]
  1. The first equivalence is just the definition of \(S(r)\) applied to each endpoint: \(u\in S(r)\) means \(y_u\ge r\), and likewise for \(v\).
  2. The middle equivalence is the defining property of the minimum: \(\min\{y_u,y_v\}\ge r\) holds precisely when both \(y_u\ge r\) and \(y_v\ge r\) — if either fell below \(r\), the smaller of the two would too.
  3. The third equivalence substitutes \(x_{uv}=\min\{y_u,y_v\}\) from part (b), and the last is the definition of \(E(r)\).

Chaining these gives \(u,v\in S(r)\iff(u,v)\in E(r)\): the edge \((u,v)\) survives in \(E(r)\) if and only if both its endpoints survive in \(S(r)\). In graph terms, \(E(r)\) is exactly the edge set induced by \(S(r)\), i.e. \(E(r)=E_{S(r)}\).
