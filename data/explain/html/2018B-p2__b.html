True. We show the output is an independent set, bound its size from below, and compare to the trivial upper bound on the optimum.

Output is independent: whenever a vertex \(v\) is added to \(S\), all of its neighbors are removed from \(G\), so no later pick can be adjacent to \(v\). Hence \(S\) has no internal edges.

Trivial ceiling: an independent set is a subset of vertices, so \(\mathrm{OPT}\le |V|\).

Lower bound on \(|S|\): let \(k\) be the number of iterations. Each iteration adds exactly one vertex to \(S\) and removes \(v\) together with its neighbors, at most \(1+\Delta\) vertices. So \(|S|=k\) and the total removed is at most \(k(\Delta+1)\). The algorithm stops only when \(G\) is empty, i.e. all \(|V|\) vertices are removed, so \(k(\Delta+1)\ge |V|\), giving

\[ |S|=k\ \ge\ \frac{|V|}{\Delta+1}\ \ge\ \frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{\Delta+1}. \]

Thus \(S\) is within a factor \(\Delta+1\) of optimal. Running time is polynomial: there are at most \(|V|\) iterations, and storing the vertex degrees in a heap lets us find the minimum-degree vertex and update after each deletion in polynomial time.

Intuition. Each vertex you take knocks out at most its \(\Delta\) neighbors plus itself, so you can never fall more than a factor \(\Delta+1\) behind.
