We are given \((P)\ \min c^\top x\) s.t. \(Ax=b,\ x\ge0\) and its dual \((D)\ \max b^\top y\) s.t. \(A^\top y\le c\). Assume \((P)\) is infeasible and \((D)\) has a feasible point \(y\); we show \((D)\) is unbounded by exhibiting feasible dual points whose objective \(\to+\infty\).

  1. Get an infeasibility certificate from Farkas' Lemma. The system \(Ax=b,\ x\ge0\) is infeasible, so by (this form of) Farkas' Lemma there exists a vector \(y'\) with \[ (y')^\top A\ge 0\qquad\text{and}\qquad (y')^\top b<0.\] This \(y'\) is the direction we will move along.
  2. Build a family of dual points. For \(\lambda>0\) define \(y_\lambda=y-\lambda y'\), where \(y\) is the given feasible dual point.
  3. Check \(y_\lambda\) stays feasible for \((D)\). Compute \[ A^\top y_\lambda=A^\top(y-\lambda y')=A^\top y-\lambda A^\top y'.\] Now \(A^\top y\le c\) since \(y\) is feasible, and \(A^\top y'\ge0\) (that is \((y')^\top A\ge0\) transposed) with \(\lambda>0\), so \(-\lambda A^\top y'\le0\). Adding, \(A^\top y_\lambda\le c\): feasible for every \(\lambda>0\).
  4. Show the objective blows up. The dual objective at \(y_\lambda\) is \[ b^\top y_\lambda=b^\top(y-\lambda y')=b^\top y-\lambda\,b^\top y'.\] Since \(b^\top y'=(y')^\top b<0\), the term \(-\lambda\,b^\top y'\) is positive and grows without bound as \(\lambda\to\infty\). Hence \(b^\top y_\lambda\to+\infty\).

We have produced feasible dual solutions with arbitrarily large objective value, so \((D)\) is unbounded, as claimed.
