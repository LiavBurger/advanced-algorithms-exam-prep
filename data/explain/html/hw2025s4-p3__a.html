The relevant primal–dual pair is \((P)\ \min c^Tx\text{ s.t. }Ax=b,\ x\ge 0\) and its dual \((D)\ \max b^Ty\text{ s.t. }A^Ty\le c\). We are told \((P)\) is infeasible and \((D)\) has a feasible point \(y\); from \(y\) we build a ray along which the dual objective runs off to \(+\infty\).

  1. Farkas certificate. Since \(Ax=b,\ x\ge 0\) has no solution, the version of Farkas' Lemma from Problem 1 supplies a vector \(y'\) with \((y')^TA\ge 0\) and \((y')^Tb<0\). Transposing the row vector, these read \(A^Ty'\ge 0\) and \(b^Ty'<0\).
  2. A feasible ray. For every \(\lambda>0\) set \(y_\lambda=y-\lambda y'\). Then \[ A^Ty_\lambda=A^Ty-\lambda A^Ty'\le c, \] because \(A^Ty\le c\) (feasibility of \(y\)) and \(-\lambda A^Ty'\le 0\) (as \(\lambda>0\) and \(A^Ty'\ge 0\)). So every \(y_\lambda\) is feasible for \((D)\).
  3. Objective blows up. The dual value at \(y_\lambda\) is \[ b^Ty_\lambda=b^Ty-\lambda\,b^Ty'. \] Since \(b^Ty'<0\), the term \(-\lambda\,b^Ty'\) is positive and grows without bound as \(\lambda\to\infty\), so \(b^Ty_\lambda\to+\infty\).

We have exhibited feasible dual points of arbitrarily large objective value, so \((D)\) is unbounded.
