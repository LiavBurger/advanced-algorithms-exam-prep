Introduce a variable \(x_v\in\{0,1\}\) for each vertex, where \(x_v=1\) means \(v\) is placed in the set \(S\). The cost of \(S\) is \(\sum_{v\in V}w_v x_v\), which we minimize. Vertex \(v\) is dominated exactly when \(v\in S\) or some neighbor of \(v\) is in \(S\), i.e. when at least one vertex of the closed neighborhood \(N(v)\cup\{v\}\) is chosen; this is the constraint \(\sum_{u\in N(v)\cup\{v\}}x_u\ge 1\). The ILP is

\[\min\sum_{v\in V}w_v x_v\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \sum_{u\in N(v)\cup\{v\}}x_u\ge 1\ \ \forall v\in V,\qquad x_v\in\{0,1\}\ \ \forall v\in V.\]

Any \(0/1\) solution picks the set \(S=\{v:x_v=1\}\); the constraints say exactly that every vertex is dominated, and the objective is the total weight of \(S\). So feasible integer solutions correspond exactly to dominating sets and their weights, and the ILP optimum is the minimum-weight dominating set.

Intuition. Each vertex just needs at least one chosen vertex in its closed neighborhood, which is what "dominated" means.
