Standard form here means a maximization \(\max c^\top x\) whose constraints are all "\(\le\)" inequalities and whose variables are all sign-constrained to be \(\ge0\). The given LP violates this in three ways: it has an equality, and two variables constrained \(\le0\). We fix each.

  1. Flip the nonpositive variables. For \(x_1\le0\) substitute \(x_1\mapsto -x_1\) with the new \(x_1\ge0\); likewise \(x_3\le0\) becomes \(x_3\mapsto -x_3\) with new \(x_3\ge0\). Variable \(x_2\ge0\) is already fine. After substitution every variable satisfies \(x_1,x_2,x_3\ge0\).
  2. Rewrite the objective. Under \(x_1\mapsto-x_1,\ x_3\mapsto-x_3\), the objective \(2x_1+5x_2-3x_3\) becomes \(2(-x_1)+5x_2-3(-x_3)=-2x_1+5x_2+3x_3\).
  3. Split the equality into two inequalities. \(x_1+2x_2=3\) is equivalent to \(x_1+2x_2\le3\) and \(x_1+2x_2\ge3\). Applying \(x_1\mapsto-x_1\): \(-x_1+2x_2\le3\), and the "\(\ge\)" half \(-x_1+2x_2\ge3\) is turned into a "\(\le\)" by negating: \(x_1-2x_2\le-3\).
  4. Convert the remaining inequality. \(x_2-3x_3\le10\) with \(x_3\mapsto-x_3\) becomes \(x_2+3x_3\le10\), already of the correct form.
Standard form: \[ \max\ -2x_1+5x_2+3x_3\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \begin{aligned} &-x_1+2x_2\le3,\\ &x_1-2x_2\le-3,\\ &x_2+3x_3\le10,\\ &x_1,x_2,x_3\ge0. \end{aligned} \]
