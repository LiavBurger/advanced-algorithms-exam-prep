Goal show \((D)\) is the LP dual of the set-cover relaxation.

Strategy write the relaxation in matrix form, push it to equality standard form with slacks, apply the mechanical dual rule, then flip signs to recognize \((D)\).

Key idea once the primal is \(\min c^\top x\) s.t. \(Mx=b,\ x\ge0\), its dual is automatically \(\max b^\top y\) s.t. \(M^\top y\le c\).

Why this move going through matrix form lets you use the one dual recipe you already know instead of guessing constraint directions.

  1. Matrix form. Let \(A\) be the \(n\times m\) incidence matrix, \(A_{j,i}=1\) if \(j\in S_i\) else \(0\). Then the constraints are \(Ax\ge\mathbf1\), and the relaxation is \(\min c^\top x\) s.t. \(Ax\ge\mathbf1,\ x\ge0\).
  2. Standard form. Rewrite \(Ax\ge\mathbf1\) as \(-Ax\le-\mathbf1\), add slacks \(s\ge0\): \(-Ax+s=-\mathbf1\), i.e. \([-A\mid I]\begin{bmatrix}x\\s\end{bmatrix}=-\mathbf1\) with \(x,s\ge0\), still minimizing \(c^\top x\).
  3. Dual rule. For \(\min \hat c^\top z\) s.t. \(Mz=\hat b,\ z\ge0\), the dual is \(\max \hat b^\top y\) s.t. \(M^\top y\le\hat c\). Here \(M=[-A\mid I]\), \(\hat b=-\mathbf1\), \(\hat c=(c;\,0)\), giving \(\max -\mathbf1^\top y\) s.t. \(-A^\top y\le c\) and \(y\le0\).
  4. Flip \(y\mapsto -y\). With the new \(y\ge0\), the objective becomes \(\mathbf1^\top y=\sum_j y_j\) and \(-A^\top y\le c\) becomes \(A^\top y\le c\), i.e. \(\sum_{j:\,j\in S_i}y_j\le c_i\). That is exactly \((D)\): \(\max\sum_j y_j\) s.t. \(\sum_{j\in S_i}y_j\le c_i\ \forall i,\ y\ge0\).
