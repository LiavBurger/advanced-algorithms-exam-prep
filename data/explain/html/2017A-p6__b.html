Goal write the dual of the LP relaxation of the hitting-set program.

Strategy relax to \(x_i\ge0\), drop the useless upper bounds, then form the dual with one variable per set.

Key idea a min-LP with "\(\ge\)" constraints and \(x\ge0\) dualizes to a max-LP with "\(\le\)" constraints and \(y\ge0\), swapping the roles of objective and right-hand side.

Why this move the mechanical dual recipe is the reliable way to get the dual, and clearing the redundant \(x_i\le1\) bounds first keeps it clean.

  1. Relax and prune. The relaxation is \(\min\sum_i x_i\) s.t. \(\sum_{i\in D}x_i\ge1\), \(0\le x_i\le1\). Since we minimize and every constraint has nonnegative coefficients, no optimal \(x_i\) ever needs to exceed \(1\), so the bounds \(x_i\le1\) are redundant and drop out, leaving \(x_i\ge0\).
  2. One dual variable per constraint. Introduce \(y_D\ge0\) for each set-constraint \(D\in C\). The dual is a maximization with "\(\le\)" constraints.
  3. Dual objective. The primal right-hand sides are all \(1\), so the dual maximizes \(\sum_{D\in C}y_D\).
  4. Dual constraints. One per primal variable \(x_i\): its objective coefficient is \(1\) and it appears in constraint \(D\) exactly when \(i\in D\), giving \(\sum_{D:\,i\in D}y_D\le1\) for each \(i\in S\). So the dual is \[\max\sum_{D\in C}y_D\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \sum_{D:\,i\in D}y_D\le1\ \ \forall i\in S,\qquad y_D\ge0.\]
