Goal write "pick the most students, at most \(c_j\) from each course" as a 0/1 integer program.

Strategy give each student one yes/no variable, maximize how many are chosen, and add one capacity line per course.

Key idea "at most \(c_j\) chosen students belong to \(U_j\)" is just the linear inequality that sums the chosen students sitting in that course.

Why this move the problem is a yes/no decision per student under counting limits — the textbook shape of a binary integer program, so we translate the words straight into math.

  1. Variables. For each student \(1\le i\le n\) let \(x_i\in\{0,1\}\), with \(x_i=1\) meaning student \(a_i\) is selected. Binary values make each student an all-or-nothing choice.
  2. Objective. We want the largest selected group, so we maximize the count \(\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i\) (each chosen student adds \(1\)).
  3. Constraints. For a course \(j\), the chosen students who take it are those \(a_i\in U_j\) with \(x_i=1\), so "at most \(c_j\) of them" is \(\sum_{i:\,a_i\in U_j} x_i \le c_j\), one line per course.
  4. Full program. \[ \max \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \sum_{i:\,a_i\in U_j} x_i \le c_j \ (1\le j\le m),\qquad x_i\in\{0,1\}. \]

A student in several courses appears in several \(U_j\), so choosing them spends one unit of each of those caps at once — exactly the "counts as a representative for each" behavior the problem describes.
