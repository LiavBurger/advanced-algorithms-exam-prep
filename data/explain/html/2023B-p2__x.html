Statement (weak duality). Let \((P)\) be an LP in standard form, \(\min c^\top x\) s.t. \(Ax=b,\ x\ge 0\), and let \((D)\) be its dual, \(\max b^\top y\) s.t. \(A^\top y\le c\) (with \(y\) free). Then for every feasible \(x\) of \((P)\) and every feasible \(y\) of \((D)\), \(c^\top x\ge b^\top y\).

  1. Use dual feasibility together with \(x\ge 0\). Dual feasibility says \(A^\top y\le c\), i.e. \((A^\top y)_j\le c_j\) for every coordinate \(j\). Since \(x\ge 0\), multiplying coordinate \(j\) by \(x_j\ge 0\) preserves the direction of the inequality: \(x_j(A^\top y)_j\le x_j c_j\). Summing over \(j\) gives \[x^\top A^\top y\le x^\top c,\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad y^\top A x\le c^\top x.\]
  2. Use primal feasibility \(Ax=b\). Substitute \(Ax=b\) into the left side: \(y^\top A x=y^\top b\). Therefore \(y^\top b\le c^\top x\).
  3. Rewrite as scalars. Both sides are scalars, and \(y^\top b=b^\top y\), so \(b^\top y\le c^\top x\), which is the claim \(c^\top x\ge b^\top y\). \(\qquad\blacksquare\)

The single subtle point is step 1: the inequality \(A^\top y\le c\) may only be multiplied by \(x^\top\) without flipping because \(x\) is nonnegative — this is exactly where primal nonnegativity is used.
