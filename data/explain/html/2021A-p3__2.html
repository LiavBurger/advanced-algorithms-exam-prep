We show the output is a valid tour of cost at most \(\tfrac43\,\text{OPT}\).

First, it is a tour: merging the cycles as in part (1) produces one cycle that visits every vertex exactly once, so the output is a legal TSP tour.

Next, there are few cycles. Every cycle has at least \(3\) vertices and the cycles are disjoint over the \(n\) vertices, so their number is \(k\le n/3\).

Each merge is cheap. It replaces an edge of weight \(\ge 1\) (weights are \(1\) or \(2\)) by an edge of weight \(\le 2\), an increase of at most \(1\), and we do this once per cycle, at most \(k\le n/3\) times. So \(\text{ALG}\le\text{cost}(C)+n/3\).

Two lower bounds on OPT finish it. An optimal TSP tour is itself a cycle cover (a single cycle through all vertices), so the minimum cover is no heavier: \(\text{cost}(C)\le\text{OPT}\). Also a tour has \(n\) edges each of weight \(\ge 1\), so \(\text{OPT}\ge n\), giving \(n/3\le\text{OPT}/3\). Combining,

\[\text{ALG}\le\text{cost}(C)+\frac{n}{3}\le\text{OPT}+\frac13\text{OPT}=\frac43\,\text{OPT}.\]

Intuition. The cycle cover is a lower bound on OPT, and merging adds only \(n/3\), which is itself at most \(\text{OPT}/3\).
