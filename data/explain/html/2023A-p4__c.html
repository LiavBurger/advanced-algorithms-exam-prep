The LP from part (b) is \(\min \mathbf{1}^\top y\) s.t. \(A_G^\top y\ge\mathbf{1},\ y\ge 0\), i.e. \(\min\sum_v y_v\) subject to \(y_u+y_v\ge 1\) for every edge \((u,v)\). Adding the integrality constraint \(y_v\in\{0,1\}\) turns this into the minimum vertex cover problem.

  1. What the integral constraint means. With \(y_v\in\{0,1\}\), read \(y_v=1\) as "vertex \(v\) is chosen" and collect \(S=\{v:y_v=1\}\). The edge constraint \(y_u+y_v\ge 1\) then says at least one of \(u,v\) is chosen — exactly the requirement that the edge \((u,v)\) is covered by \(S\). Imposing this for all edges says \(S\) is a vertex cover.
  2. What the objective means. \(\min\sum_v y_v=\min|S|\), so we are looking for the smallest set of vertices touching every edge — the minimum vertex cover.
  3. The duality picture. The primal (part (a)) is fractional maximum matching, and its dual (part (b)) is fractional minimum vertex cover. LP duality gives max fractional matching \(=\) min fractional vertex cover. The LP solutions may be non-integral in general, which is why we call them "fractional."

For bipartite graphs the LPs have integral optima on both sides, so the fractional values collapse to genuine matchings and covers: maximum matching \(=\) minimum vertex cover. This is König's theorem, obtained here for free from LP duality plus integrality of the bipartite polytope.
