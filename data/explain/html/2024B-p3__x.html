Claim A graph \(G\) on \(n\) vertices has at most \(\dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}\) minimum cuts.

Strategy Use Karger's guarantee that each fixed min cut survives with probability at least \(\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\), then add these probabilities.

Key idea One run returns a single cut, so the events "returns this min cut" are disjoint and their probabilities can only sum to at most \(1\).

Why this move The hint points to Karger, and a lower bound per cut plus disjoint events is the standard way to turn a success probability into a counting bound.

  1. Per-cut guarantee. Fix any minimum cut \((A_0,B_0)\). As shown in class, one run of Karger's contraction returns it with probability at least \(\dfrac{2}{n(n-1)}\), and this holds for every minimum cut.
  2. Events are disjoint. A single run outputs exactly one cut, so "returns \((A,B)\)" for distinct cuts are mutually exclusive, and returning some min cut has probability at most \(1\).
  3. Sum over all min cuts. Letting \(M(G)\) be the set of minimum cuts, \(\displaystyle 1\ge\sum_{(A,B)\in M(G)}\Pr[\text{returns }(A,B)]\ge \frac{2}{n(n-1)}\,|M(G)|\).
  4. Rearrange. This gives \(|M(G)|\le \dfrac{n(n-1)}{2}\).

Since each min cut claims its own slice of at least \(\frac{2}{n(n-1)}\) probability and the slices cannot overlap, there is only room for \(\frac{n(n-1)}{2}\) of them.
