True. Since \(k\) divides \(n\), write \(n=km\) for some natural number \(m\). Let \(\omega\) be a root of unity of order \(k\), meaning \(\omega^k=1\). Then \[\omega^n=\omega^{km}=(\omega^k)^m=1^m=1,\] so \(\omega^n=1\), which is exactly the statement that \(\omega\) is a root of unity of order \(n\) as well.

Intuition. If \(\omega^k=1\) and \(k\) divides \(n\), then \(\omega^n\) is just \(\omega^k\) raised to a power, which stays \(1\).
