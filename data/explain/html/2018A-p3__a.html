We write minimum-cost perfect matching with one \(0/1\) variable per edge that says whether the edge is chosen, minimize the total weight of chosen edges, and force every vertex to be covered exactly once.

Variables: for each edge \(e\in E\) introduce \(x_e\in\{0,1\}\), where \(x_e=1\) means \(e\) is in the matching and \(x_e=0\) means it is not.

The integer linear program is \[\min \sum_{e\in E} w(e)\,x_e \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \forall v\in V:\ \sum_{uv\in E} x_{uv}=1,\qquad \forall e\in E:\ x_e\in\{0,1\}.\]

The objective \(\sum_{e} w(e)\,x_e\) is the total cost of the chosen edges. The constraint \(\sum_{uv\in E} x_{uv}=1\) says exactly one chosen edge touches each vertex \(v\); using equality (not \(\le 1\)) is what forbids leaving a vertex uncovered, so it enforces a perfect matching. Thus every feasible \(x\) is the indicator of a perfect matching, and minimizing the objective gives a minimum-cost one.

Intuition. One yes/no choice per edge, with "exactly one chosen edge at each vertex" pinning down a perfect matching.
