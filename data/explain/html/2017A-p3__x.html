Claim True — the described Christofides variant is a \(1.5\)-approximation for metric Path-TSP.

Strategy Show the Euler path exists, bound the tree cost by OPT, bound the matching by \(\mathrm{OPT}/2\) using two candidate matchings, then add and shortcut.

Key idea The odd vertices of \(S\), in the order OPT visits them, split OPT's edges into subpaths that two alternating matchings cover once each — so the cheaper matching costs at most \(\mathrm{OPT}/2\).

Why this move Charging a matching against OPT by walking the special vertices in OPT-order is the heart of the Christofides analysis; leaving two vertices unmatched is what adapts it from a tour to a path.

  1. Euler path exists. \(S\) is nonempty (every leaf of \(T\) is odd). Adding \(M\) flips the parity of every matched vertex, so in \(T\cup M\) exactly the two unmatched vertices of \(S\) stay odd; a connected graph with exactly two odd vertices has an Euler path.
  2. Tree cost. OPT is a Hamilton path, hence a spanning tree, and \(T\) is a minimum spanning tree, so \(\mathrm{cost}(T)\le \mathrm{cost}(\mathrm{OPT})\).
  3. Two candidate matchings. \(|S|\) is even; list its vertices \(v_1,\dots,v_{2k}\) in OPT-order. Let \(M_1\) pair \(v_1v_2, v_3v_4,\dots\) and \(M_2\) pair \(v_2v_3, v_4v_5,\dots\); each leaves exactly two vertices of \(S\) unmatched, so each is a valid candidate for \(M\).
  4. Charge to OPT. OPT contains the disjoint subpaths \(v_1\!\to\!v_2, v_2\!\to\!v_3,\dots\); by the triangle inequality each matching edge costs at most its subpath, and \(M_1,M_2\) together use each subpath once, so \(\mathrm{cost}(M_1)+\mathrm{cost}(M_2)\le \mathrm{OPT}\).
  5. Take the cheaper. Since \(M\) is minimum, \(\mathrm{cost}(M)\le\min(\mathrm{cost}(M_1),\mathrm{cost}(M_2))\le \mathrm{OPT}/2\).

So \(\mathrm{cost}(T\cup M)\le \mathrm{OPT}+\mathrm{OPT}/2=\tfrac32\mathrm{OPT}\), and shortcutting repeats only saves cost, giving a Hamilton path within \(1.5\) times optimal.
