The dual has one variable \(y_v\) per vertex \(v\in V\) (one per primal constraint), and reads \[\max \sum_{v\in V} y_v \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \forall\, uv\in E:\ y_u+y_v\le w(e),\qquad y_v\ \text{free}.\]

Dual variables: each primal constraint is the vertex equality at some \(v\), so we introduce one dual variable \(y_v\) for each \(v\in V\). Because the primal constraint is an equality, \(y_v\) is unconstrained in sign (free).

Objective: the primal right-hand sides are all \(1\), so the dual objective is \(\sum_{v\in V} 1\cdot y_v=\sum_{v} y_v\), and since the primal is a minimization the dual is a maximization.

Constraints, one per primal variable \(x_e\): edge \(x_{uv}\) appears with coefficient \(1\) in exactly the two endpoint equalities of \(u\) and \(v\), and its objective coefficient is \(w(e)\). A primal minimization with \(x_e\ge 0\) yields a \(\le\) dual constraint, namely \(y_u+y_v\le w(e)\).

Intuition. Give each vertex a potential \(y_v\); every edge caps the sum of its two endpoints' potentials by its weight, and we maximize the total.
