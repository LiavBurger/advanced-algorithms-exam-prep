Goal write the LP relaxation of the maximum-independent-set ILP.

Strategy keep the objective and constraints, and just drop the integrality requirement.

Key idea "relaxation" means we let each \(x_v\) be any real number \(\ge 0\) instead of only \(0\) or \(1\), which makes the program solvable in polynomial time.

Why this move replacing \(x_v\in\{0,1\}\) by \(x_v\ge 0\) is the standard recipe for turning any ILP into its LP relaxation.

  1. Copy the rest unchanged. Objective \(\max\sum_{v\in V}x_v\) and edge constraints \(x_u+x_v\le 1\) for every \((u,v)\in E\) stay exactly as they were.
  2. Swap integrality for nonnegativity. \(x_v\in\{0,1\}\) becomes \(x_v\ge 0\). No explicit \(x_v\le 1\) is needed: for any edge, \(x_u+x_v\le 1\) with \(x_u\ge 0\) already forces \(x_v\le 1\) (and \(G\) is connected, so every vertex lies on an edge).
  3. The LP. \[\max\sum_{v\in V}x_v\quad\text{s.t.}\quad x_u+x_v\le 1\ \ \forall (u,v)\in E,\qquad x_v\ge 0\ \ \forall v\in V.\]
