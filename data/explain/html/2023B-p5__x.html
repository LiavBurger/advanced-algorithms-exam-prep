Goal compute the transitive closure \(G^{*}\) (which pairs \((i,j)\) are joined by some directed path) in total time asymptotically below \(n^3\).

Strategy turn reachability into a single matrix power \((A+I)^n\), then compute that power fast using fast matrix multiplication together with repeated squaring.

Key idea powers of the adjacency matrix count walks — \((A^k)_{ij}\) is the number of length-\(k\) walks from \(i\) to \(j\) — and adding a self-loop at every vertex turns "length exactly \(k\)" into "length at most \(k\)".

Why this move plain reachability (BFS from every vertex) already costs about \(n^3\); to beat that we lean on fast matrix multiplication, which runs in \(O(n^{\omega})\) with \(\omega<3\), so we reshape the problem into matrix multiplications.

  1. Walks from powers. Let \(A\) be the adjacency matrix of \(G\). A fact from class: \((A^k)_{ij}\) counts the walks of length exactly \(k\) from \(i\) to \(j\), so \((A^k)_{ij}>0\) iff such a walk exists.
  2. Self-loops relax "exactly" to "at most". Let \(A'=A+I\), the adjacency matrix of \(G'\) obtained by adding a self-loop at every vertex. Then \((A')^k_{ij}>0\) iff \(G\) has a path from \(i\) to \(j\) of length at most \(k\): a self-loop lets us pad any shorter walk out to length exactly \(k\).
  3. Pick the exponent \(n\). If \(i\) can reach \(j\) at all, it can along a simple path, which visits at most \(n\) vertices and so has length at most \(n\). Hence reachability is captured exactly by \((A')^n\): put \((i,j)\in E^{*}\) iff \((A')^n_{ij}>0\).
  4. The algorithm. Compute \(A'=A+I\); compute \((A')^n\) by repeated squaring — about \(\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\) squarings, each one a fast matrix multiplication; then read \(G^{*}\) off the positive entries.
  5. Running time. Forming \(A'\) and reading off \(G^{*}\) cost \(O(n^2)\) each. The power needs \(O(\log n)\) matrix multiplications at \(O(n^{\omega})\) apiece, for \(O(n^{\omega}\log n)\) total. Since \(\omega<3\), this is below \(n^3\).

Repeated squaring is what keeps us cheap: instead of multiplying by \(A'\) some \(n\) times, we square \(O(\log n)\) times to reach \((A')^n\), and each square rides on fast matrix multiplication to stay under the \(n^3\) barrier.
