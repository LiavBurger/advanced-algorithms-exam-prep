Claim True — this is exactly strong duality.

Strategy Recognize the hypothesis as the setup of the strong duality theorem and quote its conclusion.

Key idea If the primal is feasible ("non-empty") and its objective can't run off to infinity ("bounded"), then the dual is feasible too and both optima are equal.

Why this move Any statement equating primal and dual optimal values over a nice feasible region is a duality question, so reach straight for the duality theorem.

  1. Primal has a finite optimum. Non-empty means a feasible point exists; bounded means the objective doesn't go to \(\pm\infty\). So the primal optimum is a finite number.
  2. Weak duality orders them. For any feasible primal \(x\) and dual \(y\), the dual value is at most the primal value, so the dual can't be unbounded.
  3. Strong duality closes the gap. The theorem then guarantees the dual is feasible and the two optimal values are equal, not just ordered.

The parenthetical is the same idea in reverse: an infeasible dual would force an unbounded primal, which can't happen here.
