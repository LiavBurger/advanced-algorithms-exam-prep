We instantiate part (1) for the drawn graph and then write its dual. The graph has \(V_1=\{a,b,c\}\), \(V_2=\{d,e\}\) and edges \(e_1=ad,\ e_2=ae,\ e_3=bd,\ e_4=be,\ e_5=ce\). Order the constraint rows as the vertices \(a,b,c,d,e\).

  1. Each row is a vertex; a row has a \(1\) in the columns of the edges incident to that vertex. Vertex \(a\) touches \(e_1,e_2\Rightarrow(1,1,0,0,0)\); \(b\) touches \(e_3,e_4\Rightarrow(0,0,1,1,0)\); \(c\) touches \(e_5\Rightarrow(0,0,0,0,1)\); \(d\) touches \(e_1,e_3\Rightarrow(1,0,1,0,0)\); \(e\) touches \(e_2,e_4,e_5\Rightarrow(0,1,0,1,1)\).
  2. The right-hand side is the degree cap: \(1\) for the \(V_1\)-vertices \(a,b,c\) and \(2\) for the \(V_2\)-vertices \(d,e\), giving \((1,1,1,2,2)^{\mathsf T}\). The objective vector is all-ones (maximize \(\sum_e x_e\)). With \(A\) the matrix above, the primal is \[\max\ (1,1,1,1,1)\,x\quad\text{s.t.}\quad A\,x\le(1,1,1,2,2)^{\mathsf T},\quad x_i\in\{0,1\}.\]
  3. The dual BIP swaps roles: a variable \(y_j\) per row/vertex, a constraint per column/edge. The dual objective coefficients are the primal RHS \((1,1,1,2,2)\), the dual RHS is the primal objective \((1,1,1,1,1)^{\mathsf T}\), and the constraint matrix is transposed: \[\min\ (1,1,1,2,2)\,y\quad\text{s.t.}\quad A^{\mathsf T}y\ge(1,1,1,1,1)^{\mathsf T},\quad y_j\in\{0,1\}.\]

Because the exam asks for the dual as a BIP, the dual variables are also constrained to \(\{0,1\}\); each dual constraint \(A^{\mathsf T}y\ge 1\) corresponds to one edge and forces at least one of its endpoints to be picked.
