Adding the integrality constraint \(y_v\in\{0,1\}\) to the dual LP from part (b) gives the minimum vertex cover problem. Read \(y_v=1\) as "put vertex \(v\) into the set" and let \(S=\{v:y_v=1\}\). The constraint \(A_G^\top y\ge\mathbf{1}\) says that for every edge \((u,v)\) we have \(y_u+y_v\ge 1\); with \(0/1\) values this forces at least one endpoint of each edge into \(S\), so \(S\) touches every edge, which is exactly the definition of a vertex cover. The objective \(\min\sum_v y_v=\min|S|\) asks for the smallest such set, so this is minimum vertex cover.

In terms of duality: the primal LP was fractional maximum matching and this dual LP is fractional minimum vertex cover, so LP duality gives (max fractional matching) \(=\) (min fractional vertex cover). For bipartite graphs the optima of both LPs turn out to be integral, so maximum matching equals minimum vertex cover, which is Konig's theorem.

Intuition. Forcing the dual variables to \(0/1\) turns "\(y_u+y_v\ge 1\) on every edge" into "pick an endpoint of every edge," which is precisely covering the graph with vertices.
