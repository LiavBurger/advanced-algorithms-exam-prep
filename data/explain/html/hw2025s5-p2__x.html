The algorithm greedily builds an independent set \(S\): while the graph is nonempty, pick a vertex \(v\) of largest degree, add it to \(S\), and delete \(v\) together with all its neighbors (and every incident edge); finally return \(S\). Two short claims give the approximation guarantee.

  1. \(S\) is independent. Take \(u,v\in S\) and suppose without loss of generality that \(v\) was inserted after \(u\). If \(uv\) were an edge, then when \(u\) was added the algorithm would have deleted \(v\) along with \(u\)'s neighbors, so \(v\) could not be added later — a contradiction. Hence no two vertices of \(S\) are adjacent.
  2. \(|S|\ge n/(\Delta+1)\). Every vertex has degree at most \(\Delta\), so each iteration deletes the chosen \(v\) plus at most \(\Delta\) neighbors, i.e. at most \(\Delta+1\) vertices. Starting from \(|V|=n\), it therefore takes at least \(\tfrac{n}{\Delta+1}\) iterations to empty the graph, and each iteration adds exactly one vertex to \(S\).
  3. Approximation ratio. Let \(\mathrm{OPT}\) be the size of a maximum independent set and \(\mathrm{ALG}=|S|\). Trivially \(\mathrm{OPT}\le n\), and by the previous claim \(\mathrm{ALG}\ge\tfrac{n}{\Delta+1}\ge\tfrac{\mathrm{OPT}}{\Delta+1}\). Thus the algorithm is a \(\tfrac{1}{\Delta+1}\)-approximation for Max-Independent-Set on graphs of maximum degree at most \(\Delta\).
