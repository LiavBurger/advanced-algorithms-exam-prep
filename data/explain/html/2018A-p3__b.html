The LP relaxation is obtained by replacing the integrality constraint \(x_e\in\{0,1\}\) with the real interval \(0\le x_e\le 1\). The task then asks us to drop any constraint that is implied by the others.

Naively the relaxation would read \[\min \sum_{e\in E} w(e)\,x_e \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \forall v\in V:\ \sum_{uv\in E} x_{uv} = 1,\qquad \forall e\in E:\ 0\le x_e\le 1.\] We claim the upper bounds \(x_e\le 1\) are redundant.

  1. Take any edge \(e = uv\). Its endpoint constraint at \(u\) is \(\sum_{uw\in E} x_{uw} = 1\), a sum that includes the term \(x_{uv}\).
  2. Every term in that sum is nonnegative, because the constraint \(x_{uw}\ge 0\) is kept. So \(x_{uv} \le \sum_{uw\in E} x_{uw} = 1\).
  3. Hence \(x_e\le 1\) already follows from the vertex equality plus \(x_e\ge 0\); writing it explicitly adds nothing and can be removed.

Removing the redundant upper bounds gives the clean relaxation \[\min \sum_{e\in E} w(e)\,x_e \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \forall v\in V:\ \sum_{uv\in E} x_{uv} = 1,\qquad \forall e\in E:\ x_e\ge 0.\] This is the standard matching-polytope LP: only the vertex equalities and nonnegativity remain.
