MAX-3SAT input: a 3CNF formula with \(m\) clauses over \(n\) variables, each clause an OR of three literals from distinct variables. The randomized algorithm is as simple as possible, and linearity of expectation does the work.

Key idea: a random assignment satisfies each individual clause with probability \(7/8\); summing these per-clause probabilities gives \(7m/8\) satisfied clauses in expectation, no independence needed.
  1. Algorithm. Set each variable to \(0\) or \(1\) independently and uniformly at random (a fair coin per variable).
  2. Probability one clause is satisfied. A clause of 3 literals is unsatisfied only when all three literals are false. The three literals come from distinct variables, so their truth values are independent; each literal is false with probability \(1/2\). Thus \(\Pr[\text{clause unsatisfied}] = (1/2)^3 = 1/8\), and \(\Pr[\text{clause satisfied}] = 1 - 1/8 = 7/8\).
  3. Indicators. For clause \(i\), let \(X_i\) be an indicator random variable for the event that clause \(i\) is satisfied. Then \(\mathbb{E}[X_i] = \Pr[X_i = 1] = 7/8\). The total number satisfied is \(X = \sum_{i=1}^m X_i\).
  4. Linearity of expectation. Regardless of any dependence between clauses (they may share variables), \[\mathbb{E}[X] = \sum_{i=1}^m \mathbb{E}[X_i] = \frac{7m}{8}.\]
  5. Approximation ratio. The optimum \(\mathrm{OPT}\) can satisfy at most every clause, so \(\mathrm{OPT}\le m\). Hence \(\mathbb{E}[X] = \tfrac{7}{8}m \ge \tfrac{7}{8}\mathrm{OPT}\), making this a \(7/8\)-approximation in expectation.
