The running time is \(\Theta(n^2)\). The recurrence is

\[T(n)=T\!\left(\tfrac{5n}{6}\right)+T\!\left(\tfrac{n}{10}\right)+\Theta(n^2),\]

where the \(\Theta(n^2)\) is the split cost (it dominates the \(\Theta(n)\) combine). Look at the work level by level in the recursion tree. The top level does \(\Theta(n^2)\) work. The next level does

\[\Theta\!\Big(\big(\tfrac{5n}{6}\big)^2+\big(\tfrac{n}{10}\big)^2\Big)=\Theta\!\Big(\big(\tfrac{25}{36}+\tfrac1{100}\big)n^2\Big)\approx 0.7\,n^2,\]

which is a constant factor less than the level above. The same shrinking happens at every level, so the per-level work forms a decreasing geometric series and the total is a constant times the top level. Therefore

\[T(n)=\Theta(n^2).\]

Intuition. Each level does a fixed fraction less work than the one above, so the very first level's \(n^2\) already sets the total.
