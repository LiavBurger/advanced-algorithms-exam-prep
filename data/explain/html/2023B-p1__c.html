False. No nonzero polynomial can vanish on every integer assignment, by the Schwartz–Zippel lemma.

Suppose \(f\) is a nonzero polynomial in the five variables \(x_1,\dots,x_5\), and let \(d\) be its total degree. Consider the finite set of integers \(S=\{1,2,\dots,d+1\}\), which has \(|S|=d+1\) values, and choose each \(\alpha_i\) uniformly and independently from \(S\). By the Schwartz–Zippel lemma, \[ \Pr[f(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_5)=0] \le \frac{d}{|S|} = \frac{d}{d+1} < 1. \] Since this probability is strictly less than \(1\), there must exist some choice \(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_5 \in \{1,\dots,d+1\}\) with \(f(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_5)\neq 0\). These are integer values, so \(f\) does not vanish on every integer assignment. Hence no such nonzero polynomial exists and the statement is false.

Intuition. A nonzero low-degree polynomial has only "few" roots, so a large enough grid of integers always contains a point where it is nonzero.
