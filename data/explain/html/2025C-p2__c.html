Here \(f\) is the maximum frequency: every element \(j \in \{1,\dots,n\}\) appears in at most \(f\) of the sets. The algorithm solves the dual (D) and takes the tight sets, and we show its cost is at most \(f \cdot \mathrm{OPT}\).

  1. Solve the dual LP (D) to get an optimal solution \(y^{*}\).
  2. Let \(C = \{\, S_i : \sum_{j:\,j \in S_i} y^{*}_j = c_i \,\}\), the family of sets whose constraint (2) is tight.
  3. Output \(C\).

By part (b), \(C\) is a valid set cover, so the algorithm is correct. It runs in polynomial time: solving the LP (D) is polynomial, and checking tightness of each of the \(m\) constraints is cheap.

Now bound the cost. For every set \(S_i \in C\) we have \(\sum_{j:\,j \in S_i} y^{*}_j = c_i\) by definition of \(C\), so summing over the chosen sets,

\[ \sum_{S_i \in C} c_i = \sum_{S_i \in C}\ \sum_{j:\,j \in S_i} y^{*}_j . \]

Swap the order of summation, grouping by element \(j\): the term \(y^{*}_j\) is counted once for each chosen set that contains \(j\). Writing that count as the number of \(S_i \in C\) with \(j \in S_i\),

\[ \sum_{S_i \in C}\ \sum_{j:\,j \in S_i} y^{*}_j = \sum_{j=1}^{n} \bigl|\{ S_i \in C : j \in S_i \}\bigr|\, y^{*}_j . \]

Each element \(j\) appears in at most \(f\) sets in total, hence in at most \(f\) sets of \(C\); together with \(y^{*}_j \ge 0\) this gives \(\bigl|\{ S_i \in C : j \in S_i \}\bigr|\, y^{*}_j \le f\, y^{*}_j\). Therefore

\[ \sum_{S_i \in C} c_i \;\le\; f \sum_{j=1}^{n} y^{*}_j \;=\; f \cdot \mathrm{LPOPT}, \]

since \(\sum_{j} y^{*}_j\) is the optimal value of (D), which by LP duality equals the optimum of the primal relaxation, \(\mathrm{LPOPT}\). Finally the relaxation only enlarges the feasible region of the integer program, so \(\mathrm{LPOPT} \le \mathrm{OPT}\), the optimal integral set-cover weight. Combining,

\[ \sum_{S_i \in C} c_i \;\le\; f \cdot \mathrm{LPOPT} \;\le\; f \cdot \mathrm{OPT}. \]

Thus the algorithm returns a set cover of weight at most \(f \cdot \mathrm{OPT}\), i.e. it is an \(f\)-approximation.

Intuition. Each element's dual value gets charged to every chosen set containing it, and that happens at most \(f\) times, so we overpay by a factor of at most \(f\).
