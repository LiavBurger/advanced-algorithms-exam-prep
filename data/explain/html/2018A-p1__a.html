Strassen makes \(7\) recursive multiplications of \(n/2\times n/2\) matrices, plus a constant number of additions of \(n/2\times n/2\) matrices, each addition costing \(\Theta(n^2)\). So the running time satisfies

\[T(n)=7\,T(n/2)+\Theta(n^2).\]

Apply the Master theorem with \(a=7\), \(b=2\), and combine cost \(\Theta(n^2)=\Theta(n^{k})\) with \(k=2\). Compare \(a=7\) with \(b^k=2^2=4\): since \(7>4\), i.e. \(\log_b a=\log_2 7\approx 2.807>2=k\), the recursion (the leaves) dominates, and

\[T(n)=\Theta\!\big(n^{\log_2 7}\big).\]

Intuition. Seven subproblems that only halve in size grow faster than the quadratic merge, so the bottom of the recursion tree sets the cost.
