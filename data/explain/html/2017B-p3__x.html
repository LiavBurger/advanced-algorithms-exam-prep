We are told the cover time of a graph with \(n\) vertices and \(m\) edges is at most \(2mn\), and we must show a walk from \(s\) reaches a specific vertex \(t\) within \(8mn\) steps with probability \(\ge 3/4\). The tool is Markov's inequality applied to the hitting time.

Key idea: reaching one target \(t\) is easier than covering everything, so the expected time to hit \(t\) is bounded by the cover time \(2mn\). Then Markov turns that expectation into a tail bound at \(4\times\) the mean.
  1. Define the random variable. Let \(X\) be the number of steps until the walk first reaches \(t\) (the hitting time of \(t\) from \(s\)).
  2. Bound its expectation. The cover time is the expected time to visit all vertices; visiting all vertices in particular visits \(t\). Hence the expected time to reach \(t\) is at most the cover time: \(\mathbb{E}[X] \le 2mn\).
  3. Apply Markov. \(X\ge 0\), so Markov's inequality with threshold \(8mn\) gives \[\Pr[X > 8mn] \le \frac{\mathbb{E}[X]}{8mn} \le \frac{2mn}{8mn} = \frac{1}{4}.\]
  4. Complement. Therefore the walk reaches \(t\) within \(8mn\) steps with probability \[\Pr[X \le 8mn] = 1 - \Pr[X > 8mn] \ge 1 - \frac14 = \frac34.\]

The choice \(8mn = 4\cdot 2mn\) is exactly what makes Markov yield the clean \(1/4\) failure bound.
