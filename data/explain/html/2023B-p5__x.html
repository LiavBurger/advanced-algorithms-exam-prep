Let \(A\) be the adjacency matrix of \(G\). We turn reachability into a single matrix power and compute it fast. From class, \((A^k)_{ij}\) counts the walks of length exactly \(k\) from \(i\) to \(j\), so \((A^k)_{ij}>0\) iff such a walk exists. Let \(A'=A+I\), the adjacency matrix of the graph \(G'\) obtained by adding a self-loop at every vertex. Then \((A')^k_{ij}>0\) iff \(G\) has a path from \(i\) to \(j\) of length at most \(k\): a self-loop lets us pad any shorter walk out to length exactly \(k\). If \(i\) can reach \(j\) at all, it can along a simple path of length at most \(n\), so reachability is captured exactly by \((A')^n\).

The algorithm:

  1. Compute \(A'=A+I\).
  2. Compute \((A')^n\) by repeated squaring: about \(\lceil\log_2 n\rceil\) squarings, each one a fast matrix multiplication.
  3. Return \(G^*\) with \((i,j)\in E^*\) iff \((A')^n_{ij}>0\).

Correctness holds because \((A')^n_{ij}>0\) exactly when \(i\) reaches \(j\) in \(G\). Running time: forming \(A'\) and reading off \(G^*\) each cost \(O(n^2)\); the power needs \(O(\log n)\) matrix multiplications at \(O(n^{\omega})\) each, for \(O(n^{\omega}\log n)\) total. Since \(\omega<3\), this is asymptotically below \(n^3\).

Intuition. Reachability is just "is any power entry positive", and repeated squaring plus fast matrix multiplication computes \((A+I)^n\) under the \(n^3\) barrier.
