The algorithm picks \(v_1,\dots,v_s\) independently and uniformly at random from \(\{-1,1\}^n\) and outputs \(S=\{v_1,\dots,v_s\}\), where we take \(s=O(n/\varepsilon^2)\). We show this \(S\) is \(\varepsilon\)-unbiased with probability at least \(0.99\).

Fix a nonempty subset \(I\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\). For a uniformly random \(v\in\{-1,1\}^n\), the value \(e_I(v)=\prod_{i\in I}v_i\) is a product of independent uniform \(\pm 1\) bits, so it is itself uniform on \(\{-1,1\}\) and has mean \(0\). Since \(v_1,\dots,v_s\) are independent, the Chernoff bound gives, for some constant \(c>0\),

\[\Pr\!\left[\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{s}e_I(v_j)\Big|>\varepsilon s\right]\ \le\ e^{-c\varepsilon^2 s}.\]

Choose \(s=O(n/\varepsilon^2)\) large enough that this bound falls below \(2^{-2n}\). There are fewer than \(2^n\) nonempty subsets \(I\), so by a union bound the probability that any single \(I\) violates the deviation bound is at most \(2^n\cdot 2^{-2n}=2^{-n}\), which is far below \(0.01\). Hence with probability at least \(1-2^{-n}\ge 0.99\), every nonempty \(I\) satisfies \(\big|\sum_{v\in S}e_I(v)\big|\le\varepsilon s=\varepsilon|S|\), i.e. \(S\) is \(\varepsilon\)-unbiased. Sampling \(s=O(n/\varepsilon^2)\) vectors of \(n\) bits takes polynomial time.

Intuition. Each subset-parity is a fair \(\pm1\) coin, so its average over many samples concentrates near \(0\), and the error shrinks fast enough to survive a union bound over all subsets.
