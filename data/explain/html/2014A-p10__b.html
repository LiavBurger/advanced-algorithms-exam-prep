Goal read off the vertex (point) that goes with the basis \(\{x_1,s_2\}\).

Strategy set the three non-basic variables to \(0\), then solve the two equations for the two basic variables.

Key idea a basis names which variables are "on"; everything else is \(0\), and the two equalities pin down the rest.

Why this move every basic feasible solution is just "non-basic \(=0\), solve for the basics," so this is the mechanical way to turn a basis into an actual point.

  1. Zero out the non-basics. The basis is \(\{x_1,s_2\}\), so \(x_2=x_3=s_1=0\).
  2. Solve for \(x_1\). From \(x_1+2x_2+s_1=10\): \(x_1=10-2x_2-s_1=10\).
  3. Solve for \(s_2\). From the second equation, \(s_2=2x_1+x_2-x_3-14\); substituting \(x_1\) gives \(s_2=6-3x_2-x_3-2s_1=6\).

So the vertex is \(x_1=10,\ s_2=6\), with \(x_2=x_3=s_1=0\). Both basic values are \(\ge 0\), so it is a genuine feasible corner — and these two rewritten equations are the "dictionary" simplex reads to pivot in the next parts.
