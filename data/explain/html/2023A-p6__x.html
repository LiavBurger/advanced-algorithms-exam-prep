We must compute the sumset \(A+B=\{a+b:a\in A,b\in B\}\) and, for each element \(c\), how many pairs \((a,b)\) produce it — all in \(O(n\log n)\). The key insight is that counting sum-representations is exactly polynomial multiplication, which FFT does in \(O(n\log n)\).

  1. Encode sets as polynomials. Let \(f_A(x)=\sum_{i\in A}x^{i}\) and \(f_B(x)=\sum_{j\in B}x^{j}\). These are "indicator" polynomials: the coefficient \(a_i\) of \(x^i\) in \(f_A\) is \(1\) if \(i\in A\) and \(0\) otherwise, and similarly for \(f_B\).
  2. Read off the product. In \(g=f_A\cdot f_B\), the coefficient of \(x^k\) is \(\sum_{i+j=k}a_i\,b_j\). Each term \(a_i b_j\) is \(0/1\), and equals \(1\) exactly when \(i\in A\) and \(j\in B\).
  3. Interpretation. Hence the coefficient of \(x^k\) counts precisely the number of ways to write \(k=i+j\) with \(i\in A,\ j\in B\). So \(k\in A+B\) iff this coefficient is positive, and the coefficient itself is the requested count of representations.
  4. Complexity. Since \(A,B\subseteq\{1,\dots,n\}\), both \(f_A,f_B\) have degree at most \(n\), and their product has degree at most \(2n\). The FFT-based multiplication algorithm from class multiplies two degree-\(O(n)\) polynomials in \(O(n\log n)\) time.

Algorithm: build \(f_A,f_B\) in \(O(n)\), multiply them via FFT in \(O(n\log n)\), then scan the coefficients of \(g\) — every index \(k\) with a positive coefficient is an element of \(A+B\), and its coefficient is the number of representations.
