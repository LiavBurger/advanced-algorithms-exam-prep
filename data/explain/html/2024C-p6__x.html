We show that if \((P)\) is infeasible and \((D)\) is feasible then \((D)\) is unbounded. The idea is to use Farkas' Lemma to turn the infeasibility of \((P)\) into a concrete direction, and then walk from the given feasible dual point along that direction: feasibility is preserved while the dual objective runs off to \(+\infty\).

Let \(y\) be a feasible solution for \((D)\), so \(A^\top y\le c\). Since \(Ax=b,\ x\ge0\) is infeasible, Farkas' Lemma gives a vector \(y'\) such that

\[ (y')^\top A\ge 0 \qquad\text{and}\qquad (y')^\top b<0. \]

For every \(\lambda>0\), consider the point \(y_\lambda=y-\lambda y'\). We claim \(y_\lambda\) is feasible for \((D)\). Indeed,

\[ A^\top y_\lambda = A^\top\!\left(y-\lambda y'\right)=A^\top y-\lambda A^\top y'\le c, \]

where the inequality holds because \(A^\top y\le c\) by feasibility of \(y\), and because \(A^\top y'\ge 0\) (this is \((y')^\top A\ge0\) transposed) together with \(\lambda>0\) gives \(-\lambda A^\top y'\le 0\). So \(y_\lambda\) satisfies \(A^\top y_\lambda\le c\) for every \(\lambda>0\).

Now look at the objective value at \(y_\lambda\):

\[ b^\top y_\lambda = b^\top\!\left(y-\lambda y'\right)=b^\top y-\lambda\, b^\top y'. \]

Since \(b^\top y'=(y')^\top b<0\), the term \(-\lambda\,b^\top y'\) is positive and grows without bound as \(\lambda\to\infty\). Hence \(b^\top y_\lambda\to+\infty\) while every \(y_\lambda\) stays feasible for \((D)\). Therefore \((D)\) is unbounded, as claimed.

Intuition. Farkas hands you a feasible direction that points straight uphill for the dual, so you can keep sliding along it forever and the objective never stops climbing.
