True. The expression \(\sum_{|S|=n}\prod_{i\in S}\lambda_i\) is the \(n\)-th elementary symmetric polynomial \(e_n\) in the eigenvalues \(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{2n}\), and elementary symmetric polynomials of the eigenvalues are (up to sign) the coefficients of the characteristic polynomial.

Indeed, the characteristic polynomial of \(A\) is \(\prod_{i=1}^{2n}(x-\lambda_i)=\sum_{k}(-1)^k e_k\, x^{2n-k}\), so our expression \(e_n\) equals \((-1)^n\) times the coefficient of \(x^{n}\) in the characteristic polynomial of \(A\). We saw in class an efficient algorithm that, given a matrix, computes all the coefficients of its characteristic polynomial. Running it on \(A\), we read off the coefficient of \(x^n\), multiply by \((-1)^n\), and obtain the desired value efficiently — with no need to compute the eigenvalues themselves.

Intuition. That sum is exactly one coefficient of the characteristic polynomial (up to sign), and we already know how to compute all of those coefficients fast.
