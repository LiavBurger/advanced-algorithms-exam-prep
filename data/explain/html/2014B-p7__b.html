Here the number of bins \(k\) is fixed and we want the smallest common bin capacity so all items fit. This is exactly the makespan scheduling problem: think of the \(k\) bins as \(k\) machines and each item's weight as a job's processing time; the minimum feasible capacity is the minimum achievable maximum load.

Algorithm (greedy least-loaded, as proved in pset 2 problem 3):

  1. Go through the items one by one.
  2. Place each item into the bin that currently has the smallest total load.
  3. Output the maximum load over all bins in the resulting packing as the required capacity.

Correctness / ratio: this greedy assignment was shown in class (pset 2 problem 3) to be a \(2\)-approximation for minimizing the maximum load, i.e. the maximum load it produces is at most twice the optimal minimum capacity. Reporting that maximum load therefore gives a capacity within a factor \(2\) of optimal, and all items fit by construction. So this is a \(2\)-approximation for the minimum-bin-capacity variant.

Intuition. Minimizing the largest bin load is just makespan scheduling, and always feeding the next item to the emptiest bin is known to stay within twice the best possible.
