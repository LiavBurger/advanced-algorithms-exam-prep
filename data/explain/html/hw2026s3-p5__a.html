The primal-dual pair is (P): \(\min c^Tx\) subject to \(Ax=b,\ x\ge 0\), and (D): \(\max b^Ty\) subject to \(A^Ty\le c\). We are told (P) is infeasible and (D) is feasible, and must show (D) is unbounded.

  1. Apply Farkas (from Q4). Infeasibility of (P) means the system \(Ax=b,\ x\ge 0\) has no solution. The version of Farkas' lemma proved in Question 4 then produces a vector \(y'\) with \((y')^TA\ge 0\) and \((y')^Tb<0\).
  2. Shift a feasible dual point. Let \(y\) be any feasible solution of (D), so \(A^Ty\le c\). For each \(\lambda>0\) define \(y_\lambda=y-\lambda y'\).
  3. \(y_\lambda\) stays feasible. Transposing \((y')^TA\ge 0\) gives \(A^Ty'\ge 0\), hence \(\lambda A^Ty'\ge 0\), and therefore \[A^Ty_\lambda=A^Ty-\lambda A^Ty'\le A^Ty\le c.\]
  4. The objective blows up. Its value is \(b^Ty_\lambda=b^Ty-\lambda\,b^Ty'\). Since \(b^Ty'=(y')^Tb<0\), the term \(-\lambda\,b^Ty'\) is positive and grows without bound as \(\lambda\to\infty\). Thus (D) has feasible points of arbitrarily large objective value, i.e. it is unbounded.
