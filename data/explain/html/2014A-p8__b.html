Let \(X=\sum_i X_i\) be the number of satisfied clauses, so from part (a) \(\mathbb{E}[X]=\tfrac{7m}{8}\). We want to bound \(\Pr[X<\tfrac34 m]\).

Write the event in terms of the number of unsatisfied clauses \(m-X\), which is a non-negative random variable with \(\mathbb{E}[m-X]=m-\tfrac{7m}{8}=\tfrac{m}{8}\): \[\Pr\!\left[X<\tfrac34 m\right]=\Pr\!\left[m-X>\tfrac{m}{4}\right].\] By Markov's inequality applied to the non-negative variable \(m-X\), \[\Pr\!\left[m-X>\tfrac{m}{4}\right]\le \frac{\mathbb{E}[m-X]}{m/4}=\frac{m/8}{m/4}=\frac12.\] So the probability that fewer than \(3/4\) of the clauses are satisfied is at most \(1/2\), a closed-form bound.

(We cannot use a Chernoff bound here because the clauses are not independent, since they can share variables. If one nonetheless assumed independence, Chernoff would give the much smaller bound \(2e^{-m/168}\), but that assumption is not valid.)

Intuition. On average only \(m/8\) clauses fail, so by Markov the chance of more than \(m/4\) failing (which is what "below \(3/4\)" means) is at most one half.
