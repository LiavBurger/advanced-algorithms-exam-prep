True. This is precisely the strong duality theorem.

If the primal polyhedron is bounded and non-empty, then the primal is feasible and its objective is bounded over the feasible region, so the primal has a finite optimum. By the strong duality theorem, whenever the primal is feasible and bounded the dual is also feasible and both problems have the same optimal objective value. Indeed, if the dual were infeasible then by weak duality the primal would be unbounded, which contradicts boundedness. Hence the primal and dual optima coincide, and the statement is true.

Intuition. A bounded, non-empty primal pins down a finite optimum, and strong duality guarantees the dual meets it exactly with no gap.
