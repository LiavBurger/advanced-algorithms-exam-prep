The statement is true. It follows from the class characterization of vertices of a standard-form polytope \(P=\{x:Ax=b,\ x\ge 0\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^n\), where \(A\) has \(n\) columns.

  1. Recall the theorem proved in class: a feasible point \(y\in P\) is a vertex (a basic feasible solution) if and only if the columns of \(A\) indexed by \(\operatorname{supp}(y)=\{i:y_i>0\}\) are linearly independent — equivalently, the submatrix \(A_y\) of those columns has full column rank.
  2. This associates to every vertex \(y\) a specific subset of columns, namely its support. The map "\(y\mapsto\operatorname{supp}(y)\)" is injective: if two vertices \(y,y'\) had the same support \(S\), then both would satisfy \(A_S\,z_S=b\) with \(z_i=0\) for \(i\notin S\). Since the columns in \(S\) are linearly independent, that system has at most one solution, forcing \(y=y'\).
  3. Therefore distinct vertices give distinct subsets of the \(n\) columns of \(A\). The total number of subsets of an \(n\)-element set is \(2^n\).

An injection from the set of vertices into the \(2^n\) column-subsets shows the number of vertices is at most \(2^n\). (The bound is loose — most subsets are either not independent or do not solve \(A_S z_S=b\) with \(z_S\ge 0\) — but as an upper bound it is correct.)
