Goal Write the LP dual of the knapsack relaxation from part (c).

Strategy Apply the mechanical dual recipe: each primal constraint becomes a dual variable, each primal variable becomes a dual constraint, and max flips to min.

Key idea The one capacity row gives a variable \(z\), the \(n\) bounds \(x_i\le1\) give variables \(y_i\), and the objective is built from the primal right-hand sides.

Why this move Dualizing is a fixed procedure — reading off constraint-vs-variable roles is all that's needed, no cleverness.

  1. Assign dual variables. The primal \(\max\sum_i b_i x_i\) has the capacity row \(\sum_i w_i x_i\le W\) (variable \(z\ge0\)) and the bounds \(x_i\le1\) (variables \(y_i\ge0\)). Both are \(\ge0\) since these are \(\le\) rows in a maximization.
  2. Build the dual objective. Combine the primal right-hand sides: capacity RHS \(W\) and each bound RHS \(1\), so minimize \(Wz+\sum_i y_i\).
  3. One constraint per \(x_i\). Collect \(x_i\)'s coefficients across primal rows — \(w_i\) in the capacity row and \(1\) in its own bound — and require their weighted sum to be at least the objective coefficient \(b_i\): \[w_i z + y_i \ge b_i \quad \forall i.\]
  4. Write it out. \[\min\ Wz + \sum_i y_i \quad\text{s.t.}\quad w_i z + y_i \ge b_i\ \forall i,\qquad y_i\ge 0,\ z\ge 0.\] The \(\ge\) direction comes from the primal variables being \(\ge0\).
