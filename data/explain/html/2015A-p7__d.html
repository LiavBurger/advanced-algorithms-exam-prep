Introduce a variable \(w\) for \(|2x+y-3|\) and a variable \(z\) for \(|10x+y+5|\), and minimize their sum. Since \(|t|\) is the smallest number that is at least \(t\) and at least \(-t\), I bound each new variable below by its expression and by its negation:

\[\min\; w+z \quad\text{s.t.}\] \[w\ge 2x+y-3,\qquad w\ge -2x-y+3,\] \[z\ge 10x+y+5,\qquad z\ge -10x-y-5,\] \[w,z\ge 0.\]

The variables \(x,y\) are free. These constraints force \(w\ge|2x+y-3|\) and \(z\ge|10x+y+5|\); since we minimize \(w+z\), at the optimum each of \(w,z\) is pushed down to exactly equal its absolute value, so the LP has the same optimum as the original problem.

Intuition. Minimizing a variable kept above both \(t\) and \(-t\) squeezes it down to \(|t|\).
