Goal Give a randomized algorithm that, with probability at least \(0.9\), finds \(u\in\{0,1\}^n\) with \(d(u,v)\ge n/3\) for every \(v\in S\), where \(|S|\le n^{2024}\).

Strategy Just pick \(u\) uniformly at random; show each fixed \(v\) is close only with tiny probability, then union bound over all of \(S\).

Key idea A random \(u\) sits at expected distance \(n/2\) from any fixed \(v\), and Chernoff makes a drop to \(n/3\) exponentially unlikely — small enough to survive a polynomial-size union bound.

Why this move "Avoid every member of a not-too-large set with good probability" is the classic Chernoff-plus-union-bound pattern: bound one bad event sharply, then sum over all of them.

  1. Algorithm. Set each coordinate \(u_i\) to \(0\) or \(1\) independently with probability \(1/2\).
  2. Expected distance. Fix \(v\in S\) and let \(X_i\) be an indicator random variable for \(u_i\ne v_i\). Since \(v_i\) is fixed and \(u_i\) is a fair coin, \(\mathbb{E}[X_i]=1/2\), so \(d(u,v)=\sum_i X_i\) has \(\mathbb{E}[d(u,v)]=n/2\).
  3. One \(v\) is rarely close. The \(X_i\) are independent, so Chernoff applies. Being at distance \(\le n/3\) means dropping \(n/6\) below the mean, so \(\Pr[d(u,v)\le n/3]\le e^{-cn}\) for a constant \(c>0\) — the error shrinks fast.
  4. Union bound. Failure means some \(v\in S\) is too close, so \(\Pr[\text{fail}]\le |S|\cdot e^{-cn}\le n^{2024}\cdot e^{-cn}\).

The polynomial \(n^{2024}\) is swamped by the exponentially small \(e^{-cn}\), so for large \(n\) the failure probability is below \(0.1\) and the random \(u\) works with probability at least \(0.9\).
