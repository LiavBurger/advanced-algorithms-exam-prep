The integer program IP1 is the minimum vertex cover problem. To see this, read off what each ingredient of the program means on the graph \(G=(V,E)\).

  1. The variables. There is one binary variable \(x_v\in\{0,1\}\) per vertex \(v\in V\). Interpret \(x_v=1\) as "\(v\) is selected into the set" and \(x_v=0\) as "\(v\) is left out." So a \(0/1\) assignment is exactly a choice of a subset \(S=\{v : x_v=1\}\subseteq V\).
  2. The constraints. For each edge \(uv\in E\) we require \(x_u+x_v\ge 1\). Since the variables are \(0/1\), this holds iff at least one of \(u,v\) is selected. In words: every edge has at least one endpoint in \(S\) — precisely the definition of \(S\) being a vertex cover.
  3. The objective. Minimizing \(\sum_{v\in V} x_v\) minimizes \(|S|\), the number of selected vertices.
Choosing a \(0/1\) point that covers every edge while minimizing the count of chosen vertices is exactly asking for a smallest vertex cover. Hence IP1 computes the minimum vertex cover of \(G\).
