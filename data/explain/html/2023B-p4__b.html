The converse fails, because the degree constraints only force in-degree \(1\) and out-degree \(1\) at each vertex; they never force the chosen edges to form a single connected tour. So a union of several disjoint cycles is also feasible.

Concretely, take six vertices with two vertex-disjoint directed triangles, \(1\to 2\to 3\to 1\) and \(4\to 5\to 6\to 4\). Set \(x_{u,v}=1\) on these six edges and \(0\) on all others. In each triangle every vertex has exactly one chosen incoming edge and one chosen outgoing edge, so every incoming sum and every outgoing sum equals \(1\) and all variables are in \(\{0,1\}\); the solution is feasible.

But this is not a Hamiltonian cycle: a Hamiltonian cycle is one cycle passing through all vertices, whereas here the vertices split into two separate cycles and the subgraph is not even connected. So a feasible IP solution need not correspond to a Hamiltonian cycle.

Intuition. Counting one edge in and one out at each vertex can't tell a single big loop apart from several small ones.
