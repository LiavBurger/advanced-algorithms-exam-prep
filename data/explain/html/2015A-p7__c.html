In part (a), after the first pivot, set the non-basic variables to \(0\): \(x_1=x_3=x_4=0\) and \(s_3=0\). This gives \(x_2=10\), \(s_1=2\), \(s_2=37\). All variables are non-negative, so this is a feasible solution of the primal maximization LP, and its objective value is

\[2x_1+4x_2+3x_3+x_4=4\cdot 10=40.\]

Since the primal is a maximization and we have a feasible point of value \(40\), the primal optimum is at least \(40\). By the strong duality theorem the dual optimum equals the primal optimum, so the dual optimum is also at least \(40\).

Intuition. One feasible primal point already gives value \(40\), and duality passes that bound straight to the dual.
