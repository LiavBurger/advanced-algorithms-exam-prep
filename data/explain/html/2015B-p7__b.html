The LP relaxation keeps the same objective and constraints but drops the integrality requirement, replacing \(x_v\in\{0,1\}\) by \(x_v\ge 0\):

\[\max\sum_{v\in V}x_v\quad\text{s.t.}\quad x_u+x_v\le 1\ \ \forall (u,v)\in E,\qquad x_v\ge 0\ \ \forall v\in V.\]

Since \(G\) is connected, every vertex lies on some edge, and each such edge constraint together with \(x_u\ge 0\) already forces \(x_v\le 1\); so no separate upper bound is needed and the fractional values stay in \([0,1]\).

Intuition. Relaxing just lets a vertex be picked "fractionally" instead of all-or-nothing.
