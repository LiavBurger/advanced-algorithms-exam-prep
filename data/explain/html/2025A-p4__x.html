Goal Give a randomized poly-time algorithm that, with probability at least \(0.99\), outputs an \(\varepsilon\)-unbiased set \(S\subseteq\{-1,1\}^n\) of size \(O(n/\varepsilon^2)\).

Strategy Just sample \(S\) at random, then show every subset-parity sum is small using Chernoff on each \(I\) and a union bound over all \(I\).

Key idea For a random \(v\), each parity \(e_I(v)=\prod_{i\in I}v_i\) is a fair \(\pm1\) coin with mean \(0\), so its average over many samples concentrates near \(0\).

Why this move "Small deviation for every one of exponentially many events" is the textbook Chernoff-plus-union-bound pattern, and random sampling is the natural way to build an unbiased set.

  1. The algorithm. Pick \(v_1,\dots,v_s\) independently and uniformly from \(\{-1,1\}^n\) and output \(S=\{v_1,\dots,v_s\}\), with \(s=O(n/\varepsilon^2)\).
  2. Each parity is a fair coin. Fix a nonempty \(I\). For uniform \(v\), \(e_I(v)\) is a product of independent uniform \(\pm1\) bits, hence itself uniform on \(\{-1,1\}\) with mean \(0\).
  3. Chernoff for one \(I\). Since the \(v_j\) are independent, \(\displaystyle\Pr\Big[\big|\sum_{j=1}^s e_I(v_j)\big|>\varepsilon s\Big]\le e^{-c\varepsilon^2 s}\) for some constant \(c>0\).
  4. Choose the sample size. Take \(s=O(n/\varepsilon^2)\) large enough that this bound drops below \(2^{-2n}\).
  5. Union bound over all \(I\). There are fewer than \(2^n\) nonempty subsets, so the chance any one fails is at most \(2^n\cdot 2^{-2n}=2^{-n}\), which is far below \(0.01\).

So with probability at least \(1-2^{-n}\ge 0.99\) every parity sum stays within \(\varepsilon|S|\), meaning the random sample is already an \(\varepsilon\)-unbiased set, and drawing \(O(n^2/\varepsilon^2)\) bits runs in polynomial time.
