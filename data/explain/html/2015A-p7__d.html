Goal Model \(\min\;|2x+y-3|+|10x+y+5|\) as a linear program.

Strategy Replace each absolute value by a fresh variable bounded below by the expression and its negation, then minimize the sum.

Key idea \(|t|\) is the smallest number that is at least \(t\) and at least \(-t\).

Why this move Absolute value is not linear, but under a minimization it can be squeezed from above by a new variable that the objective automatically pushes down to the true value.

  1. Name the pieces. Let \(w\) stand for \(|2x+y-3|\) and \(z\) for \(|10x+y+5|\); the objective becomes the linear \(\min\;w+z\).
  2. Sandwich each one. Force \(w\) and \(z\) above the expression and its negation: \[w\ge 2x+y-3,\quad w\ge -2x-y+3,\quad z\ge 10x+y+5,\quad z\ge -10x-y-5.\]
  3. Why it's exact. These give \(w\ge|2x+y-3|\) and \(z\ge|10x+y+5|\); since we minimize \(w+z\), each is driven down to equal its absolute value at the optimum.
  4. Finish. Add \(w,z\ge0\); \(x,y\) stay free. The full LP is \(\min\;w+z\) subject to the four constraints above.
