Take the dual from (b) and make it a binary integer program by requiring \(y_D\in\{0,1\}\) for every \(D\in C\):

\[\max\ \sum_{D\in C}y_D\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \sum_{D:\,i\in D}y_D\le 1\ \ \text{for all }i\in S,\qquad y_D\in\{0,1\}\ \ \text{for all }D\in C.\]

Now read off what each piece means. With \(y_D\in\{0,1\}\), setting \(y_D=1\) means we choose the subset \(D\); let the chosen collection be \(\{D:y_D=1\}\). The objective \(\sum_{D\in C}y_D\) counts how many subsets we choose, and we maximize that count. The constraint \(\sum_{D:\,i\in D}y_D\le 1\) says that for every element \(i\in S\), at most one chosen subset contains \(i\); equivalently, no two chosen subsets share an element, so the chosen subsets are pairwise disjoint.

So the combinatorial problem is: find a maximum-size collection of subsets from \(C\) that are mutually (pairwise) disjoint.

Intuition. Grab as many of the given subsets as possible without ever reusing an element.
