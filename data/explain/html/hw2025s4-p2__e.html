We read off the signs of the coordinates of \(\tilde f=\tilde c'-\tilde c\), combining the facts established in parts (a) and (c).

  1. \(\tilde f_t<0\). Here \(\tilde f_t=\tilde c'_t-\tilde c_t\); part (c) gives \(\tilde c'_t<0\) and part (a) gives \(\tilde c_t=0\) (because \(t\in B\)), so \(\tilde f_t=\tilde c'_t<0\).
  2. \(\tilde f_s>0\). Here \(\tilde f_s=\tilde c'_s-\tilde c_s\); since \(s\) is fickle, part (c) gives \(\tilde c'_s\ge0\), and part (a) gives \(\tilde c_s<0\), so \(\tilde f_s=\tilde c'_s-\tilde c_s>0\).
  3. \(\tilde f_j\ge0\) for fickle \(j\in B\) with \(j\neq t\). Part (c) gives \(\tilde c'_j\ge0\), and \(\tilde c_j=0\) because \(j\in B\); hence \(\tilde f_j\ge0\).
  4. \(\tilde f_j=0\) for non-fickle \(j\in B\). A non-fickle basic variable never leaves the basis during the cycle, so it also lies in \(B'\). Then \(\tilde c'_j=\tilde c_j=0\) (both are basic reduced costs), giving \(\tilde f_j=0\).
