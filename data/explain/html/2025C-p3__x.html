Claim after poly-many steps the lazy walk reaches \(t\) with probability at least \(\tfrac1{2n}\).

Strategy write the lazy walk's transition matrix through the normalized adjacency matrix \(A\), check its eigenvalues stay in \([-\tfrac13,1]\) with a spectral gap, then reuse the mixing theorem from class verbatim.

Key idea laziness \(C=\tfrac13 I+\tfrac23 A\) only rescales eigenvalues to \(\lambda_i=\tfrac13+\tfrac23\mu_i\); this keeps the top one at \(1\) and lifts the most negative one up to \(\ge-\tfrac13\), so nothing sits near \(-1\).

Why this move the class mixing theorem needs top eigenvalue \(1\) and every other of magnitude bounded below \(1\); an ordinary walk can have an eigenvalue near \(-1\) (bipartite-like oscillation), and the lazy step is exactly the fix.

  1. Class facts about \(A\). For \(G\) regular and connected, the eigenvalues of the normalized adjacency matrix satisfy \(-1\le\mu_n\le\cdots\le\mu_1=1\), with a gap \(\mu_2\le 1-\tfrac1{4n^5}\).
  2. Lazy transition matrix. Staying with prob. \(\tfrac13\) and moving to a uniform neighbor with prob. \(\tfrac23\) gives \(C=\tfrac13 I+\tfrac23 A\). Sharing eigenvectors with \(A\), it has eigenvalues \(\lambda_i=\tfrac13+\tfrac23\mu_i\).
  3. Top eigenvalue unchanged. \(\lambda_1=\tfrac13+\tfrac23\cdot 1=1\) — the uniform/stationary direction survives.
  4. Gap preserved. \(\lambda_2=\tfrac13+\tfrac23\mu_2\le 1-\tfrac1{6n^5}\), still bounded below \(1\).
  5. Laziness kills the bad end. From \(\mu_n\ge-1\), \(\lambda_n=\tfrac13+\tfrac23\mu_n\ge-\tfrac13\). So every eigenvalue lies in \([-\tfrac13,1]\), meaning \(|\lambda_i|\le|\lambda_2|\) for all \(i>1\) — no eigenvalue near \(-1\) to spoil convergence.
  6. Conclude. With top eigenvalue \(1\) and all others of magnitude \(\le 1-\tfrac1{6n^5}\), the class theorem applies unchanged: after \(\text{poly}(n)\) steps the distribution is close to uniform, so the chance of sitting at \(t\) is roughly \(\tfrac1n\), hence at least \(\tfrac1{2n}\).
