Goal write and solve the recurrence for Strassen's matrix multiplication.

Strategy read the recursive structure off the algorithm, then apply the Master Theorem.

Key idea Strassen makes \(7\) half-size multiplications (not \(8\)); the extra additions are only \(\Theta(n^2)\) work per level.

Why this move a divide-and-conquer cost with equal-size subproblems is the exact shape the Master Theorem is built for.

  1. Recurrence. The \(7\) recursive products of \(\tfrac n2\times\tfrac n2\) matrices give \(7T(n/2)\); the \(10\) matrix additions plus split/combine cost \(\Theta(n^2)\). So \(T(n)=7\,T(n/2)+\Theta(n^2)\).
  2. Master Theorem form. With \(a=7,\ b=2,\ k=2\), compare \(a\) to \(b^k=4\). Here \(a=7>4\), the leaf-heavy case, so \(T(n)=\Theta\!\big(n^{\log_b a}\big)\).
  3. Evaluate. \(\log_2 7\approx 2.807\), giving \(T(n)=\Theta\!\big(n^{\log_2 7}\big)\) — below the \(n^3\) of naive multiplication. Intuitively the many leaves at the bottom of the tree dominate the total.
