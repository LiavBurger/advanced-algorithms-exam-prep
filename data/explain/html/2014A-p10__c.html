Goal find which non-basic variable enters the basis on the first pivot.

Strategy rewrite the objective using only the non-basic variables and pick the one whose coefficient is most positive.

Key idea a positive coefficient means raising that variable raises the objective, so the biggest coefficient is the steepest climb.

Why this move at a vertex the basic variables are locked to the non-basics; writing the objective in the free variables shows exactly which direction pays off.

  1. Substitute \(x_1\). Using \(x_1=10-2x_2-s_1\), the objective becomes \(2(10-2x_2-s_1)+5x_2+2x_3=20+x_2+2x_3-2s_1\).
  2. Compare coefficients. \(x_2\) gives \(+1\), \(x_3\) gives \(+2\), \(s_1\) gives \(-2\). Only \(x_2,x_3\) improve, and \(x_3\)'s rate is larger.

So \(x_3\) enters the basis — the most positive coefficient, i.e. the best gain per unit. This is the standard Dantzig rule, and it sets up the ratio test in part (d).
