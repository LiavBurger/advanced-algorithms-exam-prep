The variable \(s_2\) leaves the basis.

We raise the entering variable \(x_3\) from \(0\), keeping the other non-basics \(x_2=s_1=0\), and see which basic variable reaches \(0\) first. The basic variables are

\[ x_1=10-2x_2-s_1=10,\qquad s_2=6-3x_2-x_3-2s_1=6-x_3 . \]

Since \(x_1\) has no \(x_3\) term it stays at \(10\) and imposes no limit. But \(s_2=6-x_3\) reaches \(0\) when \(x_3=6\), so the requirement \(s_2\ge 0\) caps \(x_3\) at \(6\) (min ratio \(6/1=6\)). Hence \(s_2\) leaves, and after the pivot \(x_3=6\) with new basis \(\{x_1,x_3\}\).

Intuition. The entering variable grows only until the first basic variable would go negative, and that one leaves.
