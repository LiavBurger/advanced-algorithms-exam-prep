Suppose that during the run the very last item that forced a new bin to be opened had weight \(1/k\). We give a lower bound of \(1-\tfrac1k\) on the load of every bin other than the one opened last.

  1. What "opening a new bin" tells us. The algorithm opens a new bin for an item only when the item does not fit in any existing bin. So at the moment this weight-\(1/k\) item was placed, every already-open bin had less than \(1/k\) of free space — otherwise the item would have gone there instead.
  2. Translate free space into load. Free space below \(1/k\) means each of those bins was filled to more than \(1-\tfrac1k\); at least \(1-\tfrac1k\) of every such bin was already occupied.
  3. Loads only grow afterwards. Since this was the last bin-opening event, any items placed after it are merely added into existing bins, so no bin's load ever decreases. Every bin except the last-opened therefore keeps a load of at least \(1-\tfrac1k\) at the end.
Hence in the final packing, each bin other than the one opened last contains items of total weight at least \(1-\tfrac1k\).
