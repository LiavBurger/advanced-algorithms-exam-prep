True. Let \(\mathcal{F}\) be the family of edge-sets that form the cuts of \(G\), over the ground set \(E\); the weight of a cut is the sum of the weights of its edges. Each edge gets an independent uniform weight from \(\{1,\dots,4|E|\}\).

By the isolation lemma, when the weights are drawn uniformly from \(\{1,\dots,K\}\), the probability that the minimum-weight member of \(\mathcal{F}\) is not unique is at most \(|E|/K\). Here \(K=4|E|\), so this probability is at most \(|E|/(4|E|)=1/4\). Applying the same argument to the negated weights (maximizing weight is minimizing the negation), the probability that the maximum-weight cut is not unique is also at most \(1/4\).

By the union bound, the probability that at least one of these two bad events happens is at most \(1/4+1/4=1/2\). Therefore, with probability at least \(1/2\), both the minimum-weight cut and the maximum-weight cut are unique.

Intuition. Fine-grained random weights isolate a single extremal set; there is only a quarter chance of a tie at each end, so half the time both ends are unique.
