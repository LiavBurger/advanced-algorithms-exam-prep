Claim Weak duality: for \((P)=\min c^\top x\) s.t. \(Ax=b,\ x\ge0\) and its dual \((D)=\max b^\top y\) s.t. \(A^\top y\le c\), any feasible \(x,y\) satisfy \(c^\top x\ge b^\top y\).

Strategy Chain the dual constraint through \(x\), then substitute the primal equality.

Key idea Multiplying the dual inequality \(A^\top y\le c\) by the nonnegative \(x\) keeps the inequality, and \(Ax=b\) collapses the left side to \(b^\top y\).

Why this move The primal objective involves \(c^\top x\) and the dual involves \(b^\top y\); the only bridge between them is the constraint pair, so we combine them directly.

  1. Use dual feasibility with \(x\ge 0\). Since \(A^\top y\le c\) coordinatewise and \(x\ge 0\), multiplying by \(x^\top\) preserves the direction: \(x^\top A^\top y\le x^\top c\), i.e. \(y^\top A x\le c^\top x\).
  2. Use primal feasibility. Substitute \(Ax=b\) into the left side: \(y^\top A x=y^\top b\), so \(y^\top b\le c^\top x\).
  3. Read off the result. These are scalars and \(y^\top b=b^\top y\), giving \(b^\top y\le c^\top x\), the claim.

The one place nonnegativity of \(x\) matters is step 1: that is exactly what lets the dual inequality survive being multiplied through, so every dual value sits below every primal value.
