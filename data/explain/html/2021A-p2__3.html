Goal write the dual BIP of the general primal from part 1 and say in words what it means.

Strategy transpose the primal — one dual variable per course, one dual constraint per student — and read off the objective and rows.

Key idea duality turns the students' packing problem into a covering problem over courses: pick cheapest courses that reach every student.

Why this move the primal's caps \(c_j\) become the dual's costs and its right-hand ones become the dual's "\(\ge 1\)" demands, so transposing directly exposes a familiar set-cover.

  1. Variables. One binary \(y_j\in\{0,1\}\) per course \(1\le j\le m\), where \(y_j=1\) means course \(j\) is chosen; its cost is the primal cap \(c_j\).
  2. Objective. The primal's right-hand sides \(c_j\) become dual objective coefficients and the direction flips: \(\min\sum_{j=1}^{m} c_j y_j\), the total cost of chosen courses.
  3. Constraints. Each student \(a_i\) gives one row. Since \(a_i\) sits in exactly the courses \(j\) with \(a_i\in U_j\), the row is \(\sum_{j:\,a_i\in U_j} y_j\ge 1\): every student must be enrolled in at least one chosen course.
  4. Full dual. \[ \min \sum_{j=1}^{m} c_j y_j \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \sum_{j:\,a_i\in U_j} y_j\ge 1\ (1\le i\le n),\qquad y_j\in\{0,1\}. \]

Read plainly: choose courses of least total cost so that every student is covered by at least one — a weighted set-cover where courses are the sets, students are the elements, and \(c_j\) is the price of using \(U_j\).
