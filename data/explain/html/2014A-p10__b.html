A basis picks two of the five variables to be basic; the other three are non-basic and forced to \(0\). Here the basis is \(\{x_1,s_2\}\), so the non-basic variables are \(x_2=x_3=s_1=0\). The basic-feasible solution (the vertex) is obtained by solving the two equality constraints for \(x_1\) and \(s_2\) once the non-basic variables are zeroed out.

  1. Solve the first equality for the basic variable \(x_1\): from \(x_1+2x_2+s_1=10\) we get \(x_1=10-2x_2-s_1\).
  2. Solve the second equality for the basic variable \(s_2\): from \(2x_1+x_2-x_3-s_2=14\) we get \(s_2=2x_1+x_2-x_3-14\). To express \(s_2\) purely in terms of non-basic variables, substitute the \(x_1\) from step 1: \[ s_2=2(10-2x_2-s_1)+x_2-x_3-14=20-4x_2-2s_1+x_2-x_3-14=6-3x_2-x_3-2s_1 . \]
  3. Now set every non-basic variable to \(0\) (\(x_2=x_3=s_1=0\)). Step 1 gives \(x_1=10\) and step 2 gives \(s_2=6\).

Both basic values came out non-negative (\(x_1=10\ge 0,\ s_2=6\ge 0\)), so this basis is feasible and the point is a genuine vertex of the feasible polytope:

\[ x_1=10,\quad s_2=6,\quad x_2=x_3=s_1=0 . \]

The rewritten equations \(x_1=10-2x_2-s_1\) and \(s_2=6-3x_2-x_3-2s_1\) are exactly the "dictionary" the simplex algorithm will read in the next parts to decide how to pivot.
