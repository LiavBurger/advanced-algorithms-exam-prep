Every item weight is a unit fraction \(1/m\); the algorithm processes items in decreasing weight and puts each into any bin that still fits it, opening a new bin only when none does. We prove by induction on \(k\) that after all items of weight at least \(1/k\) are packed, at most \(k-1\) bins are not full.

  1. Base case \(k=2\). Items of weight at least \(1/2\) are those of weight \(1\) (each fills a bin exactly) and weight \(1/2\) (which pack two per bin). Two halves fill a bin completely, so the only possible non-full bin is one holding a single unpaired \(1/2\) item. Hence at most \(1=k-1\) non-full bin.
  2. Inductive hypothesis. Assume that after packing all items of weight at least \(1/(k-1)\), there are at most \(k-2\) non-full bins.
  3. Packing the weight-\(1/k\) items. These items are now placed. Because \(1/k\) divides \(1\), the algorithm can keep dropping \(1/k\) items into a bin until it is filled to exactly \(1\); it never has to open a new bin while any bin it is currently filling still has room. So among the bins opened during this phase, every one except possibly the last-opened ends up completely full.
The number of non-full bins is therefore at most the \(k-2\) from before plus at most one newly opened non-full bin, giving \((k-2)+1=k-1\) non-full bins after packing all items of weight at least \(1/k\). This completes the induction.
