The obstacle is that \(|\cdot|\) is not linear. The standard fix is to replace each absolute value by a fresh variable that is forced to equal it at the optimum, using the identity \(|t|=\min\{w: w\ge t\text{ and }w\ge -t\}\).

  1. Introduce \(w\) to stand for \(|2x+y-3|\) and \(z\) to stand for \(|10x+y+5|\). The objective becomes the linear \(\min\; w+z\).
  2. For each absolute value, add the two "sandwich" constraints. Since \(|t|\) is the smallest number that is \(\ge t\) and \(\ge -t\): \[w\ge 2x+y-3,\qquad w\ge -(2x+y-3)=-2x-y+3,\] \[z\ge 10x+y+5,\qquad z\ge -(10x+y+5)=-10x-y-5.\]
  3. Why this is exact: each pair of constraints forces \(w\ge|2x+y-3|\) and \(z\ge|10x+y+5|\). Because we are minimizing \(w+z\), the optimum pushes each variable down to its lower bound, i.e. \(w=|2x+y-3|\) and \(z=|10x+y+5|\). So the LP optimum equals the original objective.
  4. Sign restrictions: \(w,z\ge0\) (automatically true since they dominate a value and its negation, but stated explicitly), while \(x,y\) remain free (unrestricted) variables.
Resulting LP: \(\min\; w+z\) subject to \(w\ge 2x+y-3\), \(w\ge-2x-y+3\), \(z\ge 10x+y+5\), \(z\ge-10x-y-5\), \(w,z\ge0\).
