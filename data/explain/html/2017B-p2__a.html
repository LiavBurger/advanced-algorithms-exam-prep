The greedy algorithm sorts items by the ratio \(b_i/w_i\) and adds them in descending order while they fit. We show its approximation ratio is unbounded by exhibiting an instance where greedy does arbitrarily worse than the optimum.

Take two items and capacity \(W\) (large): item \(1\) has \(w_1=1,\ b_1=1\), and item \(2\) has \(w_2=W,\ b_2=W/2\). Their ratios are \(b_1/w_1=1\) and \(b_2/w_2=1/2\), so greedy considers item \(1\) first and takes it. After taking item \(1\) the remaining capacity is \(W-1\), which is less than \(w_2=W\), so item \(2\) no longer fits and greedy stops with total value \(1\).

The optimum instead takes item \(2\) alone, which fits since \(w_2=W\le W\), for value \(W/2\). Thus \[\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{\mathrm{greedy}}=\frac{W/2}{1}=\frac{W}{2},\] which grows without bound as \(W\to\infty\). Hence the greedy algorithm's approximation ratio is not bounded.

Intuition. Greedy grabs one tiny high-ratio item and then blocks itself from the huge item that was the real prize.
