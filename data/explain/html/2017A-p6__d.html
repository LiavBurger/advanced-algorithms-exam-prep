Goal rewrite the given LP in standard form: a \(\max\) with all "\(\le\)" constraints and all variables \(\ge0\).

Strategy fix each violation in turn — flip the nonpositive variables, split the equality into two inequalities, and orient every constraint as "\(\le\)".

Key idea a variable constrained \(\le0\) becomes \(\ge0\) by substituting its negative, and an equality is just two opposite inequalities.

Why this move standard form has a fixed checklist of requirements, so you simply repair whatever fails one item at a time.

  1. Flip the nonpositive variables. Substitute \(x_1\mapsto -x_1\) (so the new \(x_1\ge0\)) and \(x_3\mapsto -x_3\) (new \(x_3\ge0\)); \(x_2\ge0\) already. Now all variables are \(\ge0\).
  2. Rewrite the objective. Under those flips, \(2x_1+5x_2-3x_3\) becomes \(-2x_1+5x_2+3x_3\).
  3. Split the equality. \(x_1+2x_2=3\) is \(x_1+2x_2\le3\) and \(x_1+2x_2\ge3\). Applying \(x_1\mapsto-x_1\): the first is \(-x_1+2x_2\le3\); negate the second to get "\(\le\)": \(x_1-2x_2\le-3\).
  4. Convert the last inequality. \(x_2-3x_3\le10\) with \(x_3\mapsto-x_3\) becomes \(x_2+3x_3\le10\). Final standard form: \[\max\ -2x_1+5x_2+3x_3\ \ \text{s.t.}\ \ -x_1+2x_2\le3,\ \ x_1-2x_2\le-3,\ \ x_2+3x_3\le10,\ \ x_1,x_2,x_3\ge0.\]
