Standard form here means a maximization in which every constraint is a "\(\le\)" inequality and every variable is \(\ge 0\). We repair each part of the given LP that breaks one of these requirements.

First fix the variable signs. We have \(x_1\le 0\) and \(x_3\le 0\), while \(x_2\ge 0\) is already fine. Substitute \(x_1\mapsto -x_1\) and \(x_3\mapsto -x_3\), so the new \(x_1,x_3\) are \(\ge 0\). Under this substitution the objective \(2x_1+5x_2-3x_3\) becomes \(-2x_1+5x_2+3x_3\).

Next handle the equality \(x_1+2x_2=3\). An equality is the same as the two inequalities \(x_1+2x_2\le 3\) and \(x_1+2x_2\ge 3\). Applying \(x_1\mapsto -x_1\): the first becomes \(-x_1+2x_2\le 3\); the second becomes \(-x_1+2x_2\ge 3\), and multiplying by \(-1\) to make it "\(\le\)" gives \(x_1-2x_2\le -3\).

Finally the inequality \(x_2-3x_3\le 10\) is already "\(\le\)"; applying \(x_3\mapsto -x_3\) turns it into \(x_2+3x_3\le 10\). Collecting everything, the standard form is

\[\max\ -2x_1+5x_2+3x_3\quad\text{s.t.}\quad -x_1+2x_2\le 3,\ \ x_1-2x_2\le -3,\ \ x_2+3x_3\le 10,\ \ x_1,x_2,x_3\ge 0.\]

Intuition. Flip the negative variables, split the equality into two one-sided bounds, and point every inequality the same way.
