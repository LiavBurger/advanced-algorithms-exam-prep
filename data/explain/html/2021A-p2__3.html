We transpose the general primal from part 1: one dual variable per course and one dual constraint per student. Let \(y_j\in\{0,1\}\) for each course \(1\le j\le m\), where \(y_j=1\) means course \(j\) is chosen; the cost of choosing it is the primal cap \(c_j\). The primal's right-hand sides become the dual objective and the direction flips, so we minimize the total cost \(\sum_{j=1}^{m} c_j y_j\). Each student \(a_i\) gives one row: since \(a_i\) sits in exactly the courses \(j\) with \(a_i\in U_j\), the row is \(\sum_{j:\,a_i\in U_j} y_j\ge 1\), forcing every student into at least one chosen course. The full dual is

\[ \min \sum_{j=1}^{m} c_j y_j \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \sum_{j:\,a_i\in U_j} y_j\ge 1\ (1\le i\le n),\qquad y_j\in\{0,1\}. \]

In words: choose courses of least total cost so that every student is covered by at least one chosen course. This is a weighted set cover where the courses are the sets, the students are the elements, and \(c_j\) is the price of using \(U_j\).

Intuition. Duality turns the students' packing problem into a covering problem over courses: buy the cheapest courses that reach everyone.
