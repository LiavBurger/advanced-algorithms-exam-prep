Let \(M\) be a maximal matching and \(N\) a maximum matching; we show \(|M|\ge\tfrac12|N|\), which is a \(2\)-approximation. Take any edge \(e'\in N\). At least one endpoint of \(e'\) must be an endpoint of some edge of \(M\): otherwise both endpoints of \(e'\) are untouched by \(M\), so \(M\cup\{e'\}\) would still be a matching, contradicting the maximality of \(M\).

Map each edge \(e'\in N\) to one of its endpoints that lies in \(V(M)\), the set of vertices touched by \(M\). Because the edges of \(N\) are pairwise vertex-disjoint, distinct edges of \(N\) map to distinct vertices, so this map is injective. Hence \(|N|\le|V(M)|\). Since \(M\) has \(|M|\) edges it touches exactly \(2|M|\) vertices, so \(|V(M)|=2|M|\), giving \(|N|\le 2|M|\), i.e. \(|M|\ge\tfrac12|N|\). As \(M\) also cannot exceed the maximum, any maximal matching is within a factor \(2\) of the maximum matching.

Intuition. Maximality means every optimal edge is blocked by a vertex \(M\) already uses, and \(M\)'s few edges only supply \(2|M|\) such vertices.
