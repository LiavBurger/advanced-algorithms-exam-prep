The LP relaxation replaces \(x_e\in\{0,1\}\) by \(x_e\ge 0\), and we may drop the constraints \(x_e\le 1\) because the vertex equalities already imply them: \[\min \sum_{e\in E} w(e)\,x_e \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \forall v\in V:\ \sum_{uv\in E} x_{uv}=1,\qquad \forall e\in E:\ x_e\ge 0.\]

To relax, we swap integrality \(x_e\in\{0,1\}\) for the interval \(0\le x_e\le 1\), keeping the vertex equalities \(\sum_{uv\in E} x_{uv}=1\).

The upper bounds \(x_e\le 1\) are redundant. Take an edge \(e=uv\). The equality at endpoint \(u\) is \(\sum_{uw\in E} x_{uw}=1\), and it contains the term \(x_{uv}\). All the other terms are \(\ge 0\), so \(x_{uv}\le 1\) follows automatically from the equality together with \(x_e\ge 0\).

Since every \(x_e\le 1\) is already implied, we remove them, leaving only the vertex equalities and \(x_e\ge 0\).

Intuition. An edge value cannot beat the sum at its endpoint, which equals \(1\), so the upper bound comes for free.
