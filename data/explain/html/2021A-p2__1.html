We give each student one yes/no variable, maximize how many students are chosen, and add one capacity line per course. For each student \(1\le i\le n\) let \(x_i\in\{0,1\}\), where \(x_i=1\) means student \(a_i\) is selected. We want the largest selected group, so we maximize the count \(\sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i\). For each course \(j\), the selected students who take it are exactly those \(a_i\in U_j\) with \(x_i=1\), and the rule "at most \(c_j\) of them" is the linear constraint \(\sum_{i:\,a_i\in U_j} x_i \le c_j\). The full program is

\[ \max \sum_{i=1}^{n} x_i \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \sum_{i:\,a_i\in U_j} x_i \le c_j \ (1\le j\le m),\qquad x_i\in\{0,1\}. \]

A student enrolled in several courses appears in several sets \(U_j\), so selecting them uses one unit of each of those caps at once.

Intuition. One yes/no switch per student, count the switches that are on, and cap how many "on" students each course may hold.
