When do I reach for this?

A random quantity has a known mean, and you want to say it is close to that mean — or that a bad deviation is rare. Pick the tool by how much you know and how sharp a bound you need.

  • Only the mean, variable \(\ge 0\) → Markov (weak, but always available).
  • Mean + variance → Chebyshev (two-sided, polynomial tail).
  • A sum of independent 0/1 (or \(\pm1\)) variables → Chernoff (exponential tail — the workhorse).
  • "How many samples to estimate a fraction within \(\varepsilon\)?" → Chernoff, solve for \(m\).
  • "Show a good object exists w.h.p." → Chernoff on one condition, then union bound over all conditions.

Core tools

MARKOV
For \(X\ge 0\): \(\displaystyle \Pr[X\ge t]\le \frac{\mathbb{E}[X]}{t}\). only the mean known; also the engine behind Las Vegas → Monte Carlo
CHEBYSHEV
\(\displaystyle \Pr\big[|X-\mathbb{E}[X]|\ge t\big]\le \frac{\mathrm{Var}(X)}{t^2}\). you can compute the variance and want a two-sided bound
CHERNOFF (0/1 sum)
For independent \(X_i\in\{0,1\}\), \(X=\sum X_i\), \(\mu=\mathbb{E}[X]\), \(0<\varepsilon\le1\): \[ \Pr\big[|X-\mu|\ge \varepsilon\mu\big]\le 2\,e^{-\varepsilon^2\mu/3}. \] a sum of many independent indicators concentrates sharply
CHERNOFF (symmetric \(\pm1\))
For independent uniform \(Y_i\in\{-1,1\}\), \(Y=\sum_{i=1}^m Y_i\): \(\displaystyle \Pr[|Y|\ge \varepsilon m]\le 2\,e^{-\varepsilon^2 m/2}\). random signs / colorings: discrepancy and balancing arguments

Worked example — the thinking

2024A P2. Estimate a CNF formula's satisfying-assignment density to within \(1/10\) with probability \(1-2^{-n}\).

  1. Turn "estimate" into a sample mean. Draw \(m\) uniform assignments; let \(X_i\) indicate "sample \(i\) satisfies". The estimator is \(\bar X=\tfrac1m\sum X_i\), with \(\mathbb{E}[\bar X]=p\) (the true density).
  2. Pick Chernoff. The \(X_i\) are independent indicators — exactly its setting. We want \(|\bar X-p|\le 1/10\).
  3. Solve for \(m\). Set the bound \(2e^{-\varepsilon^2\mu/3}\le 2^{-n}\) and solve: \(m=\Theta(n)\) samples suffice. The failure probability shrinks exponentially in \(m\), which is why a poly number of samples buys \(1-2^{-n}\).
  4. State independence. The bound is only valid because the samples are drawn independently — say it explicitly.
Theorem Chernoff + union bound pattern. To show a random object is good with high probability: (1) fix one condition (a set, a pair), bound its failure by Chernoff \(\le \delta\); (2) union-bound over all \(N\) conditions; (3) if \(N\cdot\delta<1\), a good object must exist. Multipliers: all \(k\)-subsets \(\le n^k\); all subsets/colorings \(\le 2^n\) or \(3^n\).
Traps. Chernoff needs independence — check it. Match the variable type to the right bound (\(\{0,1\}\) vs \(\{-1,1\}\), one- vs two-sided). Markov alone is far too weak for sampling — it will not give \(2^{-n}\). When union-bounding, count the conditions correctly (subsets? pairs of subsets? \(n^{2k}\)).

Checklist

  1. Identify the random sum and its mean \(\mu\).
  2. Choose the tool by what you know: mean (Markov), + variance (Chebyshev), independent sum (Chernoff).
  3. For sample counts: set the tail \(\le\) target failure, solve for \(m\).
  4. For existence: Chernoff on one condition, then union bound; show total failure \(<1\).
  5. Confirm and state independence.
