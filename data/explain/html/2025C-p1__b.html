Claim True: with \(\alpha_k\) the minimum number of multiplications to multiply two \(k\times k\) matrices, \(\alpha_{k^2}\le(\alpha_k)^2\).

Strategy Build one concrete algorithm for \(k^2\times k^2\) matrices by treating them as \(k\times k\) grids of \(k\times k\) blocks, then count its multiplications.

Key idea An optimal \(k\times k\) method used at the block level costs \(\alpha_k\) block products, and each block product is itself a \(k\times k\) multiply costing \(\alpha_k\).

Why this move To bound a minimum from above you just exhibit any valid algorithm, and block-recursion is the natural way to turn a size-\(k\) method into a size-\(k^2\) one.

  1. Block the matrices. Given two \(k^2\times k^2\) matrices \(A,B\), partition each into a \(k\times k\) grid of \(k\times k\) blocks (consistent since \(k\cdot k=k^2\)).
  2. Outer method. The product respects the block layout, so multiplying the two grids uses \(\alpha_k\) block-multiplications (block additions cost no scalar multiplications).
  3. Inner cost. Each block-multiplication is a real \(k\times k\) product, costing \(\alpha_k\) scalar multiplications.
  4. Count and conclude. Total scalar multiplications are \(\alpha_k\cdot\alpha_k=(\alpha_k)^2\). Since this is one valid method and \(\alpha_{k^2}\) is the best over all methods, \(\alpha_{k^2}\le(\alpha_k)^2\).
