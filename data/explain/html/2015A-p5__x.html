We sample points uniformly in the unit cube and count how many land inside the radius-\(1/2\) ball, whose volume is \(\pi/6\). Let \(X_i\) be an indicator random variable for the event that the \(i\)-th sample falls inside the ball; since the cube has volume \(1\), \(E[X_i]=\pi/6\). Let \(X=\sum_{i=1}^n X_i\), so \(\mu:=E[X]=n\pi/6\), and the algorithm outputs \(6X/n\), whose expectation is \(\pi\).

  1. Translate the accuracy requirement into a deviation of \(X\). We want \(\Pr[\,|6X/n-\pi|>0.1\pi\,]<\tfrac14\). Dividing the inequality inside by \(6\) gives \(|X/n-\pi/6|>0.1\pi/6\); multiplying by \(n\) gives a deviation of \(X\) from its mean of \(0.1\cdot\tfrac{n\pi}{6}=0.1\mu\). So we need the relative deviation \(\delta=0.1\).
  2. Apply the (two-sided) Chernoff bound for a sum of independent \(\{0,1\}\) variables, \(\Pr[|X-\mu|>\delta\mu]\le 2e^{-\mu\delta^2/3}\), with \(\delta=0.1\): \[ \Pr\!\left[\Bigl|\tfrac{6X}{n}-\pi\Bigr|>0.1\pi\right]\le 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{\mu(0.1)^2}{3}\right)=2\exp\!\left(-\frac{(n\pi/6)(0.01)}{3}\right)=2e^{-n\pi/1800}.\]
  3. Force this below \(1/4\). Require \(2e^{-n\pi/1800}<\tfrac14\), i.e. \(e^{-n\pi/1800}<\tfrac18\). Taking logs: \(-\tfrac{n\pi}{1800}<\ln\tfrac18=-\ln 8\), which rearranges to \(\tfrac{n\pi}{1800}>\ln 8\).
  4. Solve for \(n\): \(\displaystyle n>\frac{1800\ln 8}{\pi}\).
A number of samples \(n>\dfrac{1800\ln 8}{\pi}\) suffices (a closed-form expression, as requested).
