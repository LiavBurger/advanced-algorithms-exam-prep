Goal Prove that after packing all items of weight at least \(1/k\), at most \(k-1\) bins are not full.

Strategy Induct on \(k\); at each new weight level the items divide \(1\) evenly, so a fresh bin isn't opened until the current one is exactly full.

Key idea Because every weight is a unit fraction \(1/m\) and \(1/k\) divides \(1\), items of size \(1/k\) can only leave one partly-filled bin behind — the last one opened.

Why this move A bound that grows by one as \(k\) increases by one is the classic shape for induction on \(k\).

  1. Base case \(k=2\). Items of weight \(\ge1/2\) are the \(1\)'s (each fills a bin) and the \(1/2\)'s (two fill a bin). The only non-full bin can be one holding a single unpaired \(1/2\), so at most \(1=k-1\).
  2. Inductive hypothesis. After packing all items of weight \(\ge1/(k-1)\), assume at most \(k-2\) non-full bins.
  3. Add the \(1/k\) items. Since \(1/k\) divides \(1\), the algorithm keeps dropping \(1/k\) items into a bin until it hits exactly \(1\), never opening a new bin while the current one has room. So every bin opened in this phase, except possibly the last, ends up completely full.

That's the old \(k-2\) non-full bins plus at most one new one, giving \((k-2)+1=k-1\). The induction closes.
