True. Let \(f(x_1,\dots,x_n)\) be a nonzero polynomial over \(\mathbb{Q}\) of degree \(d\). Draw \(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n\) independently and uniformly from the set \(\{1,2,\dots,d+1\}\), which has \(d+1\) elements. By the Schwartz-Zippel lemma,

\[\Pr_{\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n\in\{1,\dots,d+1\}}\big[f(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)=0\big]\;\le\;\frac{d}{d+1}\;<\;1.\]

Since the probability of landing on a root is strictly less than \(1\), the probability that \(f(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)\neq 0\) is strictly positive. A positive-probability event over a finite set of points cannot be empty, so there exist concrete values \(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n\in\{1,\dots,d+1\}\) with \(f(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)\neq 0\).

Intuition. A degree-\(d\) polynomial has too few roots to vanish on a grid of side \(d+1\).
