Start from the LP relaxation of part (a): replace \(x_i\in\{0,1\}\) by \(0\le x_i\le 1\), giving \(\min\sum_{i\in S}x_i\) subject to \(\sum_{i\in D}x_i\ge1\) for all \(D\in C\) and \(0\le x_i\le1\).

  1. Drop the redundant upper bounds. Because we are minimizing \(\sum_i x_i\) and every "\(\ge1\)" constraint has nonnegative coefficients, no optimal solution ever needs \(x_i>1\): lowering any \(x_i\) to \(1\) keeps all constraints satisfied and does not increase the objective. So the constraints \(x_i\le1\) can be removed, leaving \(\min\sum_i x_i\) s.t. \(\sum_{i\in D}x_i\ge1\), \(x_i\ge0\).
  2. Form the dual. This is a minimization with \(\ge\) constraints and \(x\ge0\), whose dual is a maximization with \(\le\) constraints and \(y\ge0\). Introduce one dual variable \(y_D\ge0\) per primal constraint (one per set \(D\in C\)).
  3. Dual objective. The primal right-hand sides are all \(1\), so the dual objective is \(\max\sum_{D\in C}y_D\).
  4. Dual constraints (one per primal variable \(x_i\)). The primal objective coefficient of \(x_i\) is \(1\), and \(x_i\) appears in constraint \(D\) exactly when \(i\in D\). Collecting its column gives \(\sum_{D:i\in D}y_D\le1\) for every \(i\in S\).
Dual: \(\displaystyle \max\sum_{D\in C}y_D\ \ \text{s.t.}\ \ \sum_{D:\,i\in D}y_D\le1\ \ \forall i\in S,\qquad y_D\ge0\ \ \forall D\in C.\)
