The general dual is weighted vertex cover where \(V_1\)-vertices have weight \(1\) and \(V_2\)-vertices have weight \(2\). There is one binary variable \(y_v\in\{0,1\}\) per vertex, indicating whether it is chosen for the cover. For every edge \(e=(u,v)\) there is one constraint \(y_u+y_v\ge 1\), forcing at least one endpoint to be selected. The objective minimizes the total weight of selected vertices, and the weights are the caps carried over from the primal: \(1\) for \(V_1\), \(2\) for \(V_2\). The program is

\[\min\ \sum_{v\in V_1}y_v+2\sum_{v\in V_2}y_v\quad\text{s.t.}\quad y_u+y_v\ge 1\ \ \forall\, (u,v)\in E,\qquad y_v\in\{0,1\}.\]

So the dual asks for the cheapest set of vertices touching every edge, charging \(1\) per \(V_1\)-vertex and \(2\) per \(V_2\)-vertex.

Intuition. Cover every edge with an endpoint, where \(V_2\)-vertices cost twice as much as \(V_1\)-vertices.
