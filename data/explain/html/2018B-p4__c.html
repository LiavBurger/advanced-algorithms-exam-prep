We must justify the recurrence \(\mathbb{E}[T(n)] \le \frac{2}{n}\sum_{k=n/2}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}[T(k)] + \Theta(n)\). The idea: condition on which element becomes the pivot, then bound each case by the largest subproblem it could create.

  1. Condition on the pivot's rank. The pivot is uniform, so for each \(k\in\{1,\dots,n\}\) it is the \(k\)-th largest element with probability \(\tfrac1n\). Partitioning costs \(\Theta(n)\) regardless.
  2. Bound the recursive call. If the pivot is the \(k\)-th largest, the two sides have sizes \(k-1\) and \(n-k\). Selection recurses on only one side, but we don't know which; to get an upper bound we pessimistically assume it recurses on the larger side, costing \(\max\big(\mathbb{E}[T(k-1)],\,\mathbb{E}[T(n-k)]\big)\).
  3. Average over the pivot. By the law of total expectation, \[\mathbb{E}[T(n)] \le \frac1n\sum_{k=1}^{n}\max\big(\mathbb{E}[T(k-1)],\mathbb{E}[T(n-k)]\big) + \Theta(n).\]
  4. Collapse the max. As \(k\) runs over \(1,\dots,n\), the two arguments \(k-1\) and \(n-k\) each range over \(0,\dots,n-1\). The larger of the pair is always some index in the upper half \(\{\lceil n/2\rceil,\dots,n-1\}\). Each such index is hit by at most two values of \(k\) (once as \(k-1\), once as \(n-k\)), so the sum of maxima is at most \(2\sum_{k=n/2}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}[T(k)]\).

Substituting gives \(\mathbb{E}[T(n)] \le \frac{2}{n}\sum_{k=n/2}^{n-1}\mathbb{E}[T(k)] + \Theta(n)\), as required. (Rounding of \(n/2\) is ignored per the instructions.)
