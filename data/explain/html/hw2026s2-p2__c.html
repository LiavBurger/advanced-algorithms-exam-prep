This is the expander mixing lemma. The strategy is to expand the quadratic form \(\mathbf{1}_S^{\top}A\,\mathbf{1}_T\) from part (b) in an orthonormal eigenbasis of \(A\): the top eigenvalue reproduces the "expected" term \(d|S||T|/n\), and the remaining eigenvalues, all bounded by \(\lambda\) in absolute value, control the error via Cauchy–Schwarz.

  1. Top eigenvector. As \(G\) is \(d\)-regular, each row of \(A\) sums to \(d\), so \(A\mathbf{1}=d\mathbf{1}\); hence \(u_1:=\mathbf{1}/\sqrt{n}\) is a unit eigenvector with eigenvalue \(\lambda_1=d\). Because \(A\) is real symmetric, extend to an orthonormal eigenbasis \(u_1,\dots,u_n\) with \(\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_n\) (spectral theorem).
  2. Coordinates. Write \(\mathbf{1}_S=\sum_i a_i u_i\) and \(\mathbf{1}_T=\sum_i b_i u_i\). Then \(a_1=\langle\mathbf{1}_S,u_1\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt n}\sum_{u\in S}1=\frac{|S|}{\sqrt n}\), and similarly \(b_1=\frac{|T|}{\sqrt n}\). Parseval gives \(\sum_i a_i^2=\|\mathbf{1}_S\|^2=|S|\), so the tail satisfies \(\sum_{i\ge 2}a_i^2=|S|-a_1^2\le|S|\), and likewise \(\sum_{i\ge2}b_i^2\le|T|\).
  3. Diagonalize the form. Using orthonormality and \(Au_i=\lambda_i u_i\), part (b) gives \[ |E(S,T)|=\mathbf{1}_S^{\top}A\,\mathbf{1}_T=\sum_i\lambda_i a_i b_i=\lambda_1 a_1 b_1+\sum_{i\ge2}\lambda_i a_i b_i. \] The leading term is \(\lambda_1 a_1 b_1=d\cdot\frac{|S|}{\sqrt n}\cdot\frac{|T|}{\sqrt n}=\frac{d|S||T|}{n}\).
  4. Bound the tail. By definition of \(\lambda=\max\{\lambda_2,|\lambda_n|\}\), every \(i\ge2\) has \(|\lambda_i|\le\lambda\). Hence, by Cauchy–Schwarz, \[ \Big|\sum_{i\ge2}\lambda_i a_i b_i\Big|\le\lambda\sum_{i\ge2}|a_i b_i|\le\lambda\sqrt{\textstyle\sum_{i\ge2}a_i^2}\,\sqrt{\textstyle\sum_{i\ge2}b_i^2}\le\lambda\sqrt{|S||T|}. \]
\[ \Big|\,|E(S,T)|-\tfrac{d|S||T|}{n}\,\Big|=\Big|\sum_{i\ge2}\lambda_i a_i b_i\Big|\le\lambda\sqrt{|S||T|}. \]
