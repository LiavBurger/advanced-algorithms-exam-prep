We compute \(H_n x\) with a divide-and-conquer recursion, exactly like FFT, exploiting the block structure of the Walsh–Hadamard matrix \[H_n=\begin{bmatrix} H_{n-1} & H_{n-1}\\ H_{n-1} & -H_{n-1}\end{bmatrix},\qquad N=2^n.\]

  1. Split the input. Write \(x=(x_0,x_1)\) where \(x_0\) is the top half and \(x_1\) the bottom half, each of length \(2^{n-1}=N/2\).
  2. Use the block formula. Multiplying out the \(2\times 2\) block matrix by the stacked halves, \[H_n x=\begin{bmatrix} H_{n-1} & H_{n-1}\\ H_{n-1} & -H_{n-1}\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} x_0\\ x_1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix} H_{n-1}x_0+H_{n-1}x_1\\ H_{n-1}x_0-H_{n-1}x_1\end{bmatrix}.\] The crucial point: only two distinct smaller products appear, \(H_{n-1}x_0\) and \(H_{n-1}x_1\). We do not recompute them for each block.
  3. Recurse then combine. Recursively compute the two half-size products \(H_{n-1}x_0\) and \(H_{n-1}x_1\) (each on a vector of length \(N/2\)). Then form the top half by adding them and the bottom half by subtracting them; this combining is \(N/2\) additions and \(N/2\) subtractions, i.e. \(O(N)\) work.
  4. Base case. At \(n=1\), \(H_1=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{bmatrix}\) and \(H_1 x\) is computed directly in \(O(1)\).

Running time. Let \(T(N)\) be the cost on length-\(N\) input. We make two recursive calls on size \(N/2\) plus \(O(N)\) combining, so \[T(N)=2\,T(N/2)+O(N).\] By the master theorem (the classic FFT/merge-sort recurrence), \(T(N)=O(N\log N)\), as required.
