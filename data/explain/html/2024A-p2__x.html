We want to estimate the density \(D(\varphi)\) — the fraction of the \(2^n\) assignments that satisfy \(\varphi\) — to within \(1/10\), with failure probability at most \(2^{-n}\). The idea is Monte-Carlo sampling: \(D(\varphi)\) is exactly the probability that a uniformly random assignment satisfies \(\varphi\), so estimate that probability by sampling.

  1. Algorithm. Draw \(t\) independent uniformly random assignments \(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_t\in\{0,1\}^n\), evaluate \(\varphi(\alpha_i)\) for each (checking a CNF against an assignment is fast — linear in the formula size), and output the fraction that satisfy: \(A(\varphi)=\frac1t\sum_{i=1}^{t}X_i\).
  2. Unbiasedness. Let \(X_i\) be an indicator random variable for the event "\(\alpha_i\) satisfies \(\varphi\)". Since \(\alpha_i\) is uniform, \(\mathbb{E}[X_i]=D(\varphi)\). Setting \(X=\sum_i X_i\), linearity gives \(\mathbb{E}[X]=t\,D(\varphi)\), so \(\mathbb{E}[A(\varphi)]=\mathbb{E}[X]/t=D(\varphi)\) — our estimator is centered on the right value.
  3. Concentration. The \(X_i\) are independent and bounded in \([0,1]\). The Chernoff/Hoeffding bound gives, for the bad event, \[\Pr\Big[\big|A(\varphi)-D(\varphi)\big|>\tfrac1{10}\Big]=\Pr\Big[\big|\tfrac{X}{t}-\tfrac{\mathbb{E}[X]}{t}\big|>\tfrac1{10}\Big]\le e^{-\Omega(t)}.\] (The printed line's \(\le/\ge\) direction is a typo; the quantity being bounded is the deviation exceeding \(1/10\).)
  4. Choosing \(t\). Take \(t=O(n)\) large enough that \(e^{-\Omega(t)}\le 2^{-n}\). Then the failure probability is \(\le 2^{-n}\), i.e. success \(\ge 1-2^{-n}\).

With \(t=O(n)\) samples, each evaluated in polynomial time, the whole algorithm runs in polynomial time, as required.
