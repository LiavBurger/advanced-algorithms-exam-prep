Recall the definition: a symmetric real \(A\) is positive semi-definite (PSD) when \(v^\top A v\ge 0\) for every \(v\in\mathbb{R}^n\). We must show this forces every eigenvalue to be \(\ge 0\). The trick is to feed the PSD inequality the eigenvector itself.

  1. Pick an eigenpair. Let \(\lambda\) be an eigenvalue of \(A\) with an eigenvector \(v\neq 0\), so by definition \(Av=\lambda v\).
  2. Evaluate the quadratic form at \(v\). Substitute \(Av=\lambda v\): \[v^\top A v=v^\top(\lambda v)=\lambda\,(v^\top v)=\lambda\|v\|^2,\] where \(v^\top v=\sum_i v_i^2=\|v\|^2\) is the squared Euclidean norm, and \(\lambda\) is a scalar that pulls out of the product.
  3. Combine the two facts. Since \(A\) is PSD, \(v^\top A v\ge 0\), so \(\lambda\|v\|^2\ge 0\). Because \(v\neq 0\) we have \(\|v\|^2>0\), a strictly positive number.
  4. Divide. Dividing the inequality \(\lambda\|v\|^2\ge 0\) by the positive quantity \(\|v\|^2\) preserves its direction, yielding \(\lambda\ge 0\).

As \(\lambda\) was an arbitrary eigenvalue, all eigenvalues of a PSD matrix are non-negative. (Here \(\lambda\) is guaranteed real because \(A\) is real symmetric.)
