Claim The ratio-sorted greedy knapsack has unbounded approximation ratio.

Strategy Build one tiny two-item family where greedy grabs a worthless dense item and then can't fit the valuable one; let the gap grow with \(W\).

Key idea Sorting by density \(b_i/w_i\) can make greedy commit to a high-density crumb that blocks a much more valuable heavy item.

Why this move To disprove any constant ratio, one explicit counterexample whose ratio scales with a free parameter is enough.

  1. The instance. Capacity \(W\), two items: item 1 with \(w_1=1,\ b_1=1\); item 2 with \(w_2=W,\ b_2=W/2\).
  2. Densities. Item 1 has ratio \(b_1/w_1=1\), item 2 has ratio \(b_2/w_2=(W/2)/W=1/2\). Since \(1>1/2\), greedy sorts item 1 first.
  3. What greedy does. It takes item 1 (weight \(1\le W\)), leaving capacity \(W-1
  4. What OPT does. Taking item 2 alone uses weight \(W\le W\) and yields value \(W/2\), so \(\mathrm{OPT}\ge W/2\).
  5. The ratio grows. On this instance \(\frac{\mathrm{OPT}}{\mathrm{greedy}}\ge \frac{W/2}{1}=\frac{W}{2}\). Since \(W\) can be arbitrarily large, no constant \(\rho\) survives (just pick \(W>2\rho\)), so the ratio is unbounded.
