Goal find a closed-form number of samples so the \(\pi\)-estimate is within \(10\%\) with probability at least \(3/4\).

Strategy sample points in the unit cube, count how many land in the radius-\(1/2\) ball, turn the accuracy target into a deviation bound, and force Chernoff below \(1/4\).

Key idea the fraction of points inside the ball estimates its volume \(\pi/6\), and Chernoff says the relative error stays small once you take enough samples.

Why this move the estimator is a sum of independent \(0/1\) indicators, so a Chernoff bound on its relative deviation is the direct tool.

  1. Set up the estimator. Let \(X_i\) be an indicator random variable for the \(i\)-th sample landing in the ball; the cube has volume \(1\), so \(E[X_i]=\pi/6\). With \(X=\sum_{i=1}^n X_i\), the mean is \(\mu=n\pi/6\), and the algorithm outputs \(6X/n\), whose expectation is \(\pi\).
  2. Translate accuracy into a deviation. We want \(\Pr[\,|6X/n-\pi|>0.1\pi\,]<\tfrac14\). Dividing by \(6\) and multiplying by \(n\), this is a deviation of \(X\) from \(\mu\) by \(0.1\mu\); so the relative error is \(\delta=0.1\).
  3. Apply two-sided Chernoff. For a sum of independent \(0/1\) variables, \(\Pr[|X-\mu|>\delta\mu]\le 2e^{-\mu\delta^2/3}\). With \(\delta=0.1\) and \(\mu=n\pi/6\): \[\Pr\!\left[\Bigl|\tfrac{6X}{n}-\pi\Bigr|>0.1\pi\right]\le 2e^{-(n\pi/6)(0.01)/3}=2e^{-n\pi/1800}.\]
  4. Force it below \(1/4\) and solve. Require \(2e^{-n\pi/1800}<\tfrac14\), i.e. \(e^{-n\pi/1800}<\tfrac18\); taking logs gives \(\tfrac{n\pi}{1800}>\ln 8\), so \[n>\frac{1800\ln 8}{\pi}.\] Any such \(n\) works — a closed form, no numeric evaluation needed.
