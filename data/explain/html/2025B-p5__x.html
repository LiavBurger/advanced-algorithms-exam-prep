Goal Estimate the versatility \(R(G)\) (the fraction of legal \(3\)-colorings) to within \(1/n\) with probability at least \(1-2^{-n}\), in polynomial time.

Strategy Sample many random colorings, report the fraction that are legal, and use Chernoff to show the estimate concentrates.

Key idea A uniformly random coloring is legal with probability exactly \(R(G)\), so the empirical legal-fraction is an unbiased estimate that sharpens as the sample grows.

Why this move "Estimate an unknown fraction closely with high confidence" is the textbook Monte-Carlo-plus-Chernoff setup, so sampling is the natural reach.

  1. The algorithm. Draw \(t\) independent uniform colorings \(c_1,\dots,c_t\) (each vertex colored uniformly in \(\{1,2,3\}\)). Check each for legality by scanning all edges (linear time). Output \(A(G)=\tfrac1t\sum_i X_i\), the fraction that are legal.
  2. Expectation. Let \(X_i\) be an indicator random variable for the event that \(c_i\) is legal. Then \(\mathbb E[X_i]=R(G)\), so with \(X=\sum_i X_i\) the output \(X/t\) has mean \(R(G)\) — unbiased.
  3. Concentration. The \(X_i\) are independent in \([0,1]\), so Chernoff gives \(\Pr[\,|A(G)-R(G)|\ge\tfrac1n\,]\le e^{-O(t/n^2)}\); the deviation \(1/n\) enters squared, hence the \(t/n^2\).

Take \(t=O(n^3)\): then \(t/n^2=O(n)\) and the failure probability drops below \(2^{-n}\). Sampling \(O(n^3)\) colorings and checking each in linear time is polynomial, so we get an estimate within \(1/n\) with probability at least \(1-2^{-n}\).
