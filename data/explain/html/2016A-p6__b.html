We dualize the LP relaxation of part (a), i.e. the program \(\max\sum_{uv}x_{uv}w(uv)\) subject to \(\sum_{uv\in E}x_{uv}\le 1\) for each \(v\in V\) and \(x_{uv}\ge 0\) (the integrality \(x_{uv}\in\{0,1\}\) is dropped for the relaxation).

  1. The primal is a maximization with "\(\le\)" constraints and nonnegative variables. The standard dual is then a minimization with "\(\ge\)" constraints and nonnegative dual variables.
  2. There is one dual variable per primal constraint. The primal has one constraint per vertex, so we get a variable \(y_v\ge 0\) for every \(v\in V\).
  3. There is one dual constraint per primal variable, i.e. per edge \(uv\). The column of \(x_{uv}\) in the constraint matrix has a \(1\) exactly in the two rows for its endpoints \(u\) and \(v\) (edge \(uv\) appears in vertex-\(u\)'s and vertex-\(v\)'s constraints). Hence the dual constraint is \(y_u+y_v\ge w(uv)\), where the right-hand side is the primal objective coefficient of \(x_{uv}\).
  4. The dual objective coefficients are the primal right-hand sides, all equal to \(1\); so we minimize \(\sum_{v\in V}y_v\).
\[\min\ \sum_{v\in V}y_v\quad\text{s.t.}\quad y_u+y_v\ge w(uv)\ \ \forall uv\in E,\qquad y_v\ge 0\ \ \forall v\in V.\]

By weak duality every feasible \(y\) upper-bounds the matching LP value, which is the intuition behind the covering interpretation in part (c).
