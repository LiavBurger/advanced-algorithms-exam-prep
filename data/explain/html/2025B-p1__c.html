True. Let \(X\) be the grade of a student chosen uniformly at random, so the average grade is \(\mathbb{E}[X]\), and for any threshold \(a\) the probability \(\Pr[X\ge a]\) equals the fraction of students scoring at least \(a\). Grades are non-negative, so Markov's inequality applies and gives

\[\Pr[X\ge 2\,\mathbb{E}[X]] \le \frac{\mathbb{E}[X]}{2\,\mathbb{E}[X]} = \frac12.\]

This probability is exactly the fraction of students scoring at least twice the average, so at most half of the students do.

Intuition. Since grades can't be negative, too many students can't all sit far above the average, or they would drag the average up.
