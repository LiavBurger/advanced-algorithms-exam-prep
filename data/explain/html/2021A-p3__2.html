Claim the algorithm from part (1) returns a valid tour of cost at most \(\tfrac43\,\text{OPT}\).

Strategy bound the extra cost of merging by counting the cycles, then compare the cycle cover and the edge count against OPT.

Key idea each patch swaps an edge of cost \(\ge 1\) for one of cost \(\le 2\), so it adds at most \(1\), and there are at most \(n/3\) patches.

Why this move two clean lower bounds on OPT are available — the cover is no costlier than the optimal tour, and any tour has \(n\) unit-or-more edges — so both \(\text{cost}(C)\) and \(n/3\) fold into OPT.

  1. It is a tour. Merging as in (1) yields one cycle visiting every vertex exactly once, so the output is a legal TSP tour.
  2. Few cycles. Each cycle has at least \(3\) vertices and they are disjoint over \(n\) vertices, so their count is \(k\le n/3\).
  3. Cheap patching. Each merge replaces an edge of cost \(\ge 1\) by one of cost \(\le 2\), an increase of at most \(1\); over \(\le n/3\) merges, \(\text{ALG}\le\text{cost}(C)+n/3\).
  4. Bound by OPT. The optimal tour is itself a cycle cover, so \(\text{cost}(C)\le\text{OPT}\); and any tour has \(n\) edges each costing \(\ge 1\), so \(\text{OPT}\ge n\), i.e. \(n/3\le\text{OPT}/3\).

Chaining these: \(\text{ALG}\le\text{cost}(C)+\tfrac{n}{3}\le\text{OPT}+\tfrac13\text{OPT}=\tfrac43\text{OPT}\). So the algorithm never exceeds \(\tfrac43\) times the best tour.
