Claim True: \(\displaystyle\sum_{|S|=n}\prod_{i\in S}\lambda_i\) can be computed efficiently from \(A\) without finding the eigenvalues.

Strategy Recognize the sum as an elementary symmetric polynomial of the eigenvalues, which is a coefficient of the characteristic polynomial we already know how to compute.

Key idea Symmetric functions of eigenvalues show up directly as characteristic-polynomial coefficients, so you never need the eigenvalues themselves.

Why this move A sum over all size-\(n\) subsets of products of eigenvalues is exactly \(e_n\), and "sum/product of roots" always points to the coefficient relations of the polynomial with those roots.

  1. Name the expression. The sum is the \(n\)-th elementary symmetric polynomial \(e_n(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_{2n})\).
  2. Relate to the characteristic polynomial. Since \(p(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{2n}(x-\lambda_i)\), Vieta's formulas make the coefficient of \(x^{n}\) equal to \((-1)^n e_n\).
  3. Use the known algorithm. We saw in class an efficient algorithm that computes all coefficients of the characteristic polynomial of a given matrix directly.
  4. Read off the answer. Take the coefficient of \(x^n\) and multiply by \((-1)^n\) to get \(e_n\), i.e. the desired sum — computed efficiently and with no eigenvalues needed.
