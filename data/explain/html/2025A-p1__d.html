True. The set \(\{v\in\mathbb{R}^n : Av=v\}\) is exactly the eigenspace of eigenvalue \(1\), and for a connected regular graph this eigenvalue has multiplicity exactly \(1\), so the space has dimension \(1\).

The dimension is at least \(1\): the eigenvalue \(1\) really occurs. The all-ones vector \(\mathbf{1}\) satisfies \(A\mathbf{1}=\mathbf{1}\), because in the (normalized) adjacency matrix of a regular graph every row sums to \(1\). So \(\mathbf{1}\) lies in the space and the space is nonzero.

The dimension is at most \(1\): we proved in class that \(1\) is the largest eigenvalue, and it was shown in the homework that when \(G\) is connected the second-largest eigenvalue is strictly less than \(1\). Hence \(1\) has multiplicity exactly \(1\), so no eigenvector for eigenvalue \(1\) is independent of \(\mathbf{1}\).

Therefore the eigenspace of \(1\) is the single line spanned by \(\mathbf{1}\), and \(\{v:Av=v\}\) has dimension exactly \(1\). The statement is true.

Intuition. Connectivity is exactly what prevents a second independent direction fixed by \(A\); only the all-ones direction survives.
