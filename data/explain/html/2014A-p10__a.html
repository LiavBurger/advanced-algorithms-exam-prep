We turn each inequality into an equation by adding one non-negative variable that soaks up the gap between the two sides. The "\(\le\)" constraint may fall short of its bound, so we add a slack \(s_1\ge 0\); the "\(\ge\)" constraint may overshoot, so we subtract a surplus \(s_2\ge 0\). The objective is unchanged (the new variables get coefficient \(0\)). The standard form is:

\[ \max\; 2x_1+5x_2+2x_3 \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \begin{aligned} &x_1+2x_2+s_1=10,\\ &2x_1+x_2-x_3-s_2=14,\\ &x_i,\,s_i\ge 0 . \end{aligned} \]

This is two equations in five non-negative variables, exactly the form simplex needs.

Intuition. A slack fills the room left under a "\(\le\)" and a surplus removes the excess over a "\(\ge\)", so both become equalities.
