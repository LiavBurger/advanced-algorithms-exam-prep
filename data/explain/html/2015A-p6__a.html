We prove by induction on \(k\) that after packing all items of weight at least \(1/k\), at most \(k-1\) bins are not full.

Base case \(k=2\). The items of weight at least \(1/2\) are the \(1\)'s (each fills a bin by itself) and the \(1/2\)'s (which are packed in pairs). The only way to leave a non-full bin is a single unpaired \(1/2\), so there is at most one non-full bin, which is \(k-1=1\).

Inductive step. Assume that after packing all items of weight at least \(1/(k-1)\) there are at most \(k-2\) non-full bins. Now we pack the items of weight exactly \(1/k\). Since \(1/k\) divides \(1\), the algorithm keeps placing \(1/k\) items into a bin until that bin becomes exactly full, and it never opens a new bin while some bin still has room. So every bin opened during this phase, except possibly the very last one opened, ends up completely full. Therefore this phase adds at most one new non-full bin.

Combining, the number of non-full bins is at most \((k-2)+1=k-1\), completing the induction.

Intuition. Because each weight \(1/k\) divides \(1\) evenly, a bin fills perfectly before the next one opens, so each new weight level can leave only one partly-filled bin behind.
