Goal Write the dual of the matching LP relaxation and state its variables.

Strategy Give one dual variable per vertex constraint, form the dual objective from the right-hand sides, and read off one dual constraint per primal edge variable.

Key idea Each edge variable appears in exactly its two endpoint constraints, so its dual constraint caps the sum of those two endpoint potentials by the edge weight.

Why this move Taking a dual is mechanical once you match dual variables to primal constraints and dual constraints to primal variables — the only judgment call is the sign rules.

  1. Dual variables. One per vertex constraint: \(y_v\) for each \(v\in V\). Since each primal constraint is an equality, \(y_v\) is free (unconstrained in sign).
  2. Objective. The primal right-hand sides are all \(1\), so the dual maximizes \(\sum_{v\in V} y_v\).
  3. Constraints, one per \(x_e\). Edge \(x_{uv}\) appears with coefficient \(1\) in the constraints of its endpoints \(u,v\), and has objective coefficient \(w(e)\). Primal min with \(x_e\ge 0\) gives a "\(\le\)" dual constraint: \(y_u + y_v \le w(e)\).

The dual is \[\max \sum_{v\in V} y_v \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \forall\, uv\in E:\ y_u + y_v \le w(e),\qquad y_v\ \text{free}.\] Think of each \(y_v\) as a vertex potential; every edge caps the sum of its endpoints' potentials, and we maximize the total.
