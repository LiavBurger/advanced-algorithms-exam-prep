The primal is \(\max\;c^\top x\) with \(Ax\le b,\ x\ge0\), so its dual is \(\min\;b^\top y\) with \(A^\top y\ge c,\ y\ge0\). The three primal constraints give dual variables \(y_1,y_2,y_3\ge0\), and their right-hand sides \(12,7,10\) form the dual objective. Each primal variable gives one dual constraint (read down its column of \(A\), set \(\ge\) its objective coefficient):

\[\min\; 12y_1+7y_2+10y_3 \quad\text{s.t.}\] \[3y_1+y_2+2y_3\ge 2,\] \[y_1-3y_2+y_3\ge 4,\] \[y_1+2y_2+3y_3\ge 3,\] \[4y_1+3y_2-y_3\ge 1,\] \[y_1,y_2,y_3\ge 0.\]

Intuition. Taking the dual just transposes the roles: each constraint becomes a variable and each variable becomes a constraint.
