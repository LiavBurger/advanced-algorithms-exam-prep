False. Consider the symmetric matrix

\[A=\begin{bmatrix}-1&0\\ 0&-1\end{bmatrix}=-I.\]

Its determinant is \(\det(A)=(-1)(-1)-0=1\ge 0\), so the hypothesis holds. But its eigenvalues are both \(-1\), and for any \(x\neq 0\) we have \(x^\top A x=-\lVert x\rVert^2<0\), so \(A\) is not PSD (it is in fact negative definite). This gives a symmetric matrix with nonnegative determinant that fails to be PSD, so the statement is false.

Intuition. The determinant only sees the product of eigenvalues, so two negative eigenvalues cancel in sign and hide a matrix that is the opposite of PSD.
