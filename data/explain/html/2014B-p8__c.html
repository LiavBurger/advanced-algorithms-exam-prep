We dualize LP1, the primal \(\min \sum_{v} x_v\) subject to \(x_u+x_v\ge 1\) for every edge and \(x_v\ge 0\). The dual has one variable per primal constraint and one constraint per primal variable.

  1. Dual variables. Each primal inequality is an edge constraint \(x_u+x_v\ge 1\), so we introduce one dual variable \(y_{uv}\ge 0\) per edge \(uv\in E\).
  2. Dual objective. The right-hand sides of the primal constraints are all \(1\), and the dual maximizes \(\sum_{uv\in E} 1\cdot y_{uv} = \sum_{uv\in E} y_{uv}\).
  3. Dual constraints. There is one dual constraint per primal variable \(x_v\). In the primal, \(x_v\) appears (with coefficient \(1\)) exactly in the constraints of edges incident to \(v\). Its primal objective coefficient is \(1\), giving the dual constraint \(\sum_{u\in N(v)} y_{uv} \le 1\) for every \(v\in V\).
  4. Sign. Because the primal variables satisfy \(x_v\ge 0\), the dual constraints are \(\le\); and dualizing \(\ge\) primal constraints in a min-problem yields \(y_{uv}\ge 0\).
The dual DLP1 is \[ \max \sum_{uv\in E} y_{uv}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \sum_{u\in N(v)} y_{uv}\le 1\ \ \forall\, v\in V,\qquad y_{uv}\ge 0\ \ \forall\, uv\in E. \] It puts a weight on each edge so that the weights around any vertex sum to at most \(1\), maximizing total weight — a fractional matching.
