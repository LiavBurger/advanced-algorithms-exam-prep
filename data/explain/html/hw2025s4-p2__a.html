Setup for the whole problem. We prove that the simplex method with Bland's rule (always pivot on the smallest eligible index) cannot cycle. Assume, for contradiction, that it does. Along a cycle the objective value never changes, so every basis appearing in the cycle shares the same, necessarily degenerate, basic feasible solution. A variable that enters the basis somewhere in the cycle (and hence also leaves it) is called fickle; every fickle variable stays at value \(0\) throughout the cycle. Let \(t\) be the fickle variable of largest index, and consider the pivot at some basis \(B\) where \(s\) enters and \(t\) leaves. Write \(\bar A=A_B^{-1}A\), \(\bar b=A_B^{-1}b\), and reduced costs \(\tilde c^{\,T}=c^T-c_B^TA_B^{-1}A\).
