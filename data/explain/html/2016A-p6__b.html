Goal write the dual of the LP relaxation from part (a): \(\min \sum_{v} y_v\) s.t. \(y_u+y_v\ge w(uv)\) for every edge, \(y_v\ge 0\).

Strategy apply the standard primal-dual recipe: one dual variable per primal constraint (vertex), one dual constraint per primal variable (edge), and flip max to min.

Key idea each edge variable \(x_{uv}\) appears in exactly its two endpoint constraints, so its dual constraint involves only \(y_u\) and \(y_v\).

Why this move the primal has "\(\le\)" constraints and \(x\ge 0\), which is the textbook shape whose dual is a minimization with "\(\ge\)" constraints — just read the recipe off the columns.

  1. Relax first. Drop \(x_{uv}\in\{0,1\}\) down to \(x_{uv}\ge 0\); the LP is \(\max\sum x_{uv}w(uv)\) s.t. \(\sum_{uv\ni v}x_{uv}\le 1\), \(x\ge 0\).
  2. Dual variables. One per vertex constraint: \(y_v\ge 0\) for each \(v\in V\).
  3. Dual constraints. One per edge. Edge \(uv\) sits in the constraints of \(u\) and \(v\), so \(y_u+y_v\ge w(uv)\), the objective coefficient of \(x_{uv}\).
  4. Dual objective. The primal right-hand sides are all \(1\), so we minimize \(\sum_{v\in V} y_v\).

By weak duality any feasible \(y\) upper-bounds the matching value, which is the covering picture spelled out in part (c).
