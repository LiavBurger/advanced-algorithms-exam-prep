Claim True: random edge weights in \(\{1,\dots,4|E|\}\) give both a unique minimum-weight cut and a unique maximum-weight cut with probability at least \(1/2\).

Strategy Treat cuts as sets of edges and apply the isolation lemma once for the minimum and once for the maximum, then union-bound the two failure chances.

Key idea With weights drawn from a range \(4|E|\) times the ground-set size, the isolation lemma makes a non-unique extremal set very unlikely — at most \(1/4\) each way.

Why this move "Unique minimum/maximum over a family of subsets under random weights" is the exact signature of the isolation lemma, so it is the natural tool to reach for.

  1. Model cuts as a set family. Let \(\mathcal{F}\) be the family of edge-sets that are the cuts of \(G\); the ground set is \(E\), and a cut's weight is the sum of its crossing-edge weights.
  2. Bound the min failure. Weights are uniform over \(\{1,\dots,4|E|\}\), so the range is \(4|E|\) against ground-set size \(|E|\). The isolation lemma gives that the minimum-weight cut fails to be unique with probability at most \(1/4\).
  3. Bound the max failure. Apply the same lemma to the negated weights (maximizing is minimizing the negation), so the maximum-weight cut is non-unique also with probability at most \(1/4\).
  4. Union bound. The chance that at least one of the two bad events happens is at most \(1/4+1/4=1/2\).
  5. Conclude. So with probability at least \(1/2\) both extremal cuts are unique — random weights isolate a single best and single worst cut.
