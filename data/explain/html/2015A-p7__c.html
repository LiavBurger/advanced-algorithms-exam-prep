We want a lower bound of \(40\) on the dual optimum. We get it for free by exhibiting one feasible primal solution of value \(40\) and then invoking LP duality — no dual computation needed.

  1. Part (a) already produced such a solution. After the pivot, set the non-basic variables to \(0\): \(x_1=x_3=x_4=0\) and \(s_3=0\). Then \(x_2=10\), \(s_1=2\), \(s_2=37\).
  2. Check feasibility: all of \(x_2,s_1,s_2\ge0\) and all original variables are non-negative, so every constraint of the primal \(\max\)-LP is satisfied. The objective value is \(2x_1+4x_2+3x_3+x_4=4\cdot10=40\).
  3. Because the primal is a maximization and we have a feasible point of value \(40\), the primal optimum is at least \(40\) (any feasible value is a lower bound on the max).
  4. By the strong duality theorem, the dual optimum equals the primal optimum. Therefore the dual optimum is also at least \(40\).
The logic chain: feasible primal value \(40\ \le\ \text{primal optimum}\ =\ \text{dual optimum}\). Hence the dual optimum \(\ge 40\). We only needed one concrete feasible primal solution plus strong duality.
