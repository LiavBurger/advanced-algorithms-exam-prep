Claim The integer version of the dual DLP1 is the maximum matching problem.

Strategy Add integrality \(y_{uv}\in\{0,1\}\) and read off what the picked edge set must satisfy.

Key idea The per-vertex constraint \(\sum_{u\in N(v)} y_{uv}\le1\) says at most one chosen edge touches each vertex — that is exactly a matching.

Why this move Naming an ILP is translating its ingredients to graph language, so we interpret the constraints on the chosen edges.

  1. Variables = chosen edges. \(y_{uv}\in\{0,1\}\) marks whether edge \(uv\) is picked, so a solution is a set \(M\subseteq E\).
  2. Constraints = matching. For each vertex \(v\), at most one incident edge is picked, i.e. no two chosen edges share an endpoint.
  3. Objective = most edges. Maximizing \(\sum_{uv} y_{uv}\) maximizes \(|M|\).

The largest set of pairwise non-adjacent edges is the maximum matching — the natural counterpart to the minimum vertex cover of part (a).
