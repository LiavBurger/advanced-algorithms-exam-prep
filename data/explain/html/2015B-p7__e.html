The primal LP from part (b) is \(\max\sum_{v\in V} x_v\) subject to \(x_u+x_v\le 1\) for every edge \((u,v)\in E\), and \(x\ge 0\). It is in standard maximization form (all \(\le\) constraints, \(x\ge 0\)), so its dual is a minimization with one variable per primal constraint and one constraint per primal variable.

Introduce a dual variable \(y_{uv}\ge 0\) for each edge \((u,v)\in E\). Each edge constraint has right-hand side \(1\), so the dual objective is \(\min\sum_{(u,v)\in E} y_{uv}\). Each primal variable \(x_v\) has objective coefficient \(1\) and appears with coefficient \(1\) in exactly the edge constraints touching \(v\); this gives one dual constraint per vertex \(v\).

So the dual is \[ \min \sum_{(u,v)\in E} y_{uv}\quad\text{s.t.}\quad \sum_{u:\,(u,v)\in E} y_{uv}\ge 1 \ \text{ for every } v\in V,\qquad y\ge 0. \]

Intuition. The dual spreads weight over the edges so that every vertex is "covered" with total weight at least \(1\) — a fractional edge cover, the mirror image of the primal picking vertices.
