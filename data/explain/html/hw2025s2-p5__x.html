Let \(A\) be the adjacency matrix of the directed graph \(G\) on \(n\) vertices. We want its transitive closure \(G^*\): the graph having an edge \((i,j)\) exactly when \(j\) is reachable from \(i\). The starting point, seen in class, is that \((A^k)_{i,j}\) counts the walks of length exactly \(k\) from \(i\) to \(j\).

  1. Add self-loops to turn "exactly \(k\)" into "at most \(k\)". Let \(A'=A+I\), the adjacency matrix of the graph \(G'\) obtained from \(G\) by adding a self-loop at every vertex. Then \((A'^{\,k})_{i,j}>0\) iff there is a walk from \(i\) to \(j\) of length at most \(k\) in \(G\): any \(G\)-walk of length \(\le k\) can be extended to length exactly \(k\) in \(G'\) by repeating self-loops, and conversely a positive entry witnesses such a padded walk.
  2. Length \(n\) is enough. If \(j\) is reachable from \(i\) at all, it is reachable by a simple path, which visits at most \(n\) vertices and hence has length at most \(n\). Therefore \((A'^{\,n})_{i,j}>0\) iff \(j\) is reachable from \(i\).
  3. Algorithm. Form \(A'=A+I\); compute \(A'^{\,n}\) by repeated squaring; return the graph \(G^*\) with an edge \((i,j)\) precisely when \((A'^{\,n})_{i,j}>0\).
  4. Running time. Building \(A'\) and reading off \(G^*\) each cost \(O(n^2)\). Repeated squaring computes \(A'^{\,n}\) using \(O(\log n)\) matrix multiplications, each in \(O(n^\omega)\) time by fast matrix multiplication, for a total of \(O(n^\omega\log n)\).
