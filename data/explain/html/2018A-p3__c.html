We take the dual of the relaxed primal from part (b): \[\min \sum_{e\in E} w(e)\,x_e \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \forall v\in V:\ \sum_{uv\in E} x_{uv} = 1,\qquad x_e\ge 0.\] There is one primal constraint per vertex, so the dual has one variable per vertex.

Dual variables: for each vertex \(v\in V\) introduce \(y_v\). Because its primal constraint is an equality, the dual variable \(y_v\) is unconstrained in sign (free), rather than restricted to be nonnegative.
  1. Objective. The primal right-hand sides are all \(1\) (the vertex equalities \(=1\)). The dual maximizes the inner product of right-hand sides with dual variables: \(\max \sum_{v\in V} y_v\).
  2. Constraints, one per primal variable \(x_e\). The primal variable \(x_{uv}\) appears in exactly two vertex constraints, those of its endpoints \(u\) and \(v\), each with coefficient \(1\). Its primal objective coefficient is \(w(e)\). A primal minimization with \(x_e\ge 0\) yields a dual "\(\le\)" constraint, so for edge \(e = uv\) we get \(y_u + y_v \le w(e)\).
  3. Sign of \(y_v\). An equality primal constraint dualizes to a free variable, so each \(y_v\) is unconstrained.

The dual is therefore \[\max \sum_{v\in V} y_v \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \forall\, uv\in E:\ y_u + y_v \le w(e),\qquad y_v\ \text{free}.\] Intuitively each vertex is assigned a "potential" \(y_v\); every edge caps the sum of its endpoints' potentials by its weight, and we maximize total potential.
