We form the dual by the standard recipe for a primal in the form \(\max\; c^\top x\) subject to \(Ax\le b,\ x\ge0\): the dual is \(\min\; b^\top y\) subject to \(A^\top y\ge c,\ y\ge0\). Each primal constraint gives a dual variable, and each primal variable gives a dual constraint.

  1. There are three primal constraints, so introduce dual variables \(y_1,y_2,y_3\ge0\) (one per constraint). Their right-hand sides \(12,7,10\) become the dual objective, giving \(\min\; 12y_1+7y_2+10y_3\).
  2. Each primal variable produces one dual constraint of the form "(its column of \(A\))\(\cdot y\ \ge\) (its objective coefficient)". Read the coefficients column by column from the primal.
  3. \(x_1\) (column \(3,1,2\); objective \(2\)): \(3y_1+y_2+2y_3\ge2\).
  4. \(x_2\) (column \(1,-3,1\); objective \(4\)): \(y_1-3y_2+y_3\ge4\).
  5. \(x_3\) (column \(1,2,3\); objective \(3\)): \(y_1+2y_2+3y_3\ge3\).
  6. \(x_4\) (column \(4,3,-1\); objective \(1\)): \(4y_1+3y_2-y_3\ge1\).
Dual LP: \(\min\; 12y_1+7y_2+10y_3\) subject to \(3y_1+y_2+2y_3\ge2\), \(\ y_1-3y_2+y_3\ge4\), \(\ y_1+2y_2+3y_3\ge3\), \(\ 4y_1+3y_2-y_3\ge1\), \(\ y_1,y_2,y_3\ge0\). The inequalities flip to "\(\ge\)" and constraints become variables (and vice versa).
