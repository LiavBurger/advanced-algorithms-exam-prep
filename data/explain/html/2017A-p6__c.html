Claim the BIP version of the dual is the set packing problem: pick the largest collection of pairwise disjoint subsets from \(C\).

Strategy impose \(y_D\in\{0,1\}\) on the dual and read what each piece means combinatorially.

Key idea the constraint "each element sits in at most one chosen set" is exactly the statement that the chosen sets don't overlap.

Why this move turning a \(0/1\) variable back into "chosen / not chosen" and a "\(\le 1\)" constraint into "no sharing" is the standard way to name what an ILP is really asking.

  1. Interpret the variables. With \(y_D\in\{0,1\}\), \(y_D=1\) means "subset \(D\) is chosen"; let \(\mathcal D=\{D:y_D=1\}\) be the chosen collection.
  2. Interpret the objective. \(\sum_D y_D=|\mathcal D|\) counts the chosen subsets, and we maximize it.
  3. Interpret the constraint. \(\sum_{D:\,i\in D}y_D\le1\) says each element \(i\in S\) lies in at most one chosen subset — so no two chosen subsets share an element. That is: find a maximum-size family of mutually disjoint subsets of \(C\), the set packing problem.
