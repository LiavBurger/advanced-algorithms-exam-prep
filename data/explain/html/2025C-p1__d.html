False. Being a vertex is neither necessary nor sufficient for optimality. Consider the program "minimize \(0\) subject to \(0\le x\le 1\)": the objective is constant, so every feasible point is optimal, including the interior points of \((0,1)\), which are not vertices. This breaks the "only if" direction — an optimal solution need not be a vertex.

Conversely, take "minimize \(x\) subject to \(0\le x\le 1\)": the vertex \(x=1\) is feasible but has value \(1\), while the optimum is \(0\) at \(x=0\). So a vertex need not be optimal, breaking the "if" direction. Both directions of the "iff" fail, so the statement is false. (What is actually true is the weaker fact that a feasible bounded LP always has an optimal solution that is a vertex.)

Intuition. With a flat or tilted objective, optima can fill a whole face or sit at the opposite vertex, so "optimal" and "vertex" are not the same thing.
