We show that relaxing an infeasible system \(Cx\le d\) into \(Cx\le d+2^{-L'}\mathbf 1\) keeps it infeasible. The tools are Farkas' Lemma II and the class volume bound on vertices of a standard-form polyhedron; throughout \(C\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}\), \(d\in\mathbb R^m\).

  1. A Farkas certificate. By Farkas' Lemma II, infeasibility of \(Cx\le d\) yields \(z\ge0\) with \(z^\top C=0\) and \(z^\top d<0\). Scaling \(z\) by \(1/(-z^\top d)>0\) preserves \(z\ge0\) and \(z^\top C=0\) while normalizing \(z^\top d=-1\).
  2. A nonempty standard-form polytope. Reading \(z^\top C=0\) as \(C^\top z=0\), the set \(Q=\{y\in\mathbb R^m:C^\top y=0,\ d^\top y=-1,\ y\ge0\}\) is nonempty. It is in standard form, with \(n+1\) equality constraints over \(m\) nonnegative variables. Minimizing the zero objective over \(Q\) is optimal at every point, so by the class result (a standard-form LP attaining its optimum attains it at a vertex) \(Q\) has a vertex \(\hat y\).
  3. Two facts about \(\hat y\). (i) The vertex/volume bound gives \(|\hat y_j|\le 2^{L_1}\) for every \(j\), with \(L_1\) polynomial (its proof is just Cramer's rule on the basis matrix, whose entries are input data). (ii) A vertex is a basic feasible solution, so at most \(n+1\) coordinates are nonzero. With \(\hat y\ge0\), \(\sum_j\hat y_j=\sum_j|\hat y_j|\le(n+1)2^{L_1}\).
  4. Choosing \(L'\). Pick \(L'\) polynomial and large enough that \(2^{-L'}(n+1)2^{L_1}<1\); any \(L'>L_1+\log_2(n+1)\) works.

Using \(d^\top\hat y=-1\), \[(d+2^{-L'}\mathbf 1)^\top\hat y=d^\top\hat y+2^{-L'}\sum_j\hat y_j=-1+2^{-L'}\sum_j\hat y_j<-1+1=0.\] So \(\hat y\ge0\), \(C^\top\hat y=0\), and \((d+2^{-L'}\mathbf 1)^\top\hat y<0\). By the easy direction of Farkas' Lemma II, \(\{x:Cx\le d+2^{-L'}\mathbf 1\}\) is infeasible.
