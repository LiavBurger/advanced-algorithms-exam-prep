Goal Prove some assignment always satisfies at least \(m/2\) of the \(m\) clauses.

Strategy Pair any assignment \(x\) with its bitwise negation \(\bar x\) and show every clause is caught by at least one of them.

Key idea A clause failed by \(x\) has all its literals false, so flipping every variable makes them all true — the complement \(\bar x\) satisfies it.

Why this move To force a \(\ge m/2\) guarantee, average two complementary assignments; if their satisfied counts sum to \(\ge m\), the larger clears \(m/2\).

  1. The pairing. Fix any assignment \(x\) and let \(\bar x\) flip every variable. Compare how many clauses each satisfies.
  2. Every clause is caught. If \(x\) fails a clause, all three literals are false under \(x\); flipping every variable turns them all true, so \(\bar x\) satisfies it. No clause is failed by both.
  3. Count. Summing over clauses, (clauses satisfied by \(x\)) + (clauses satisfied by \(\bar x\)) \(\ge m\), since each clause contributes at least \(1\).

If two nonnegative numbers add to at least \(m\), the bigger is at least \(m/2\). So one of \(x,\bar x\) satisfies \(\ge m/2\) clauses — such an assignment always exists.
