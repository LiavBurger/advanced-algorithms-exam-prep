To dualize \((P):\ \min c^{\top}x\ \text{s.t.}\ Ax\ge c,\ x\ge0\), the solution first rewrites \((P)\) in the equality "standard form" that the dual recipe applies to, then reads off the dual, and finally cleans it up with a sign change. The one place symmetry is used is \(A^{\top}=A\).

  1. Standard form. Flip each "\(\ge\)" constraint by multiplying by \(-1\): \(Ax\ge c\iff -Ax\le -c\). Then absorb the slack with non-negative variables \(s\ge0\) to make it an equality: \(-Ax+Is=-c\). This gives \[ \min\ c^{\top}x\quad\text{s.t.}\quad -Ax+Is=-c,\ \ x,s\ge0 . \]
  2. Apply the dual recipe. For a standard-form primal \(\min\{b^{\top}\xi:\ M\xi=d,\ \xi\ge0\}\) the dual is \(\max\{d^{\top}y:\ M^{\top}y\le b,\ y\ \text{free}\}\). Here \(\xi=(x,s)\), \(b=(c,0)\), \(M=(-A\mid I)\), \(d=-c\). So \(M^{\top}y\le b\) splits into two blocks: \(-A^{\top}y\le c\) and \(Iy\le0\), i.e. \(y\le0\); the objective is \(\max\,(-c)^{\top}y\). Using \(A^{\top}=A\): \[ \max\ -c^{\top}y\quad\text{s.t.}\quad -Ay\le c,\ \ y\le0 . \]
  3. Tidy up. Substitute \(z=-y\) (so \(y\le0\) becomes \(z\ge0\), and \(-Ay=Az\), \(-c^{\top}y=c^{\top}z\)): \[ (D)\quad \max\ c^{\top}z\quad\text{s.t.}\quad Az\le c,\ \ z\ge0 . \]
\[ (D)\qquad \max\ c^{\top}z \quad\text{s.t.}\quad Az\le c,\ \ z\ge0 . \]

Notice \((D)\) has the same data \((A,c)\) as \((P)\), just with \(\ge/\min\) swapped for \(\le/\max\): thanks to symmetry the problem is essentially self-dual. This is exactly what part (b) exploits.
