Claim The greedy algorithm is a \(2/3\)-approximation: its legally colored weight is at least \(\tfrac23\) of the optimum.

Strategy Bound the illegal weight created at each step by an average, sum over vertices so every edge is counted once, then compare to the easy bound \(\mathrm{OPT}\le w(E)\).

Key idea Choosing the lightest of three classes puts the new illegal weight at most one-third of the vertex's edges into placed vertices, so total illegal weight is at most \(\tfrac13 w(E)\).

Why this move A greedy "pick the minimum" rule always pairs with "min \(\le\) average", and charging each edge to its later endpoint is the standard way to make the per-step bounds add up cleanly.

  1. Set up the timeline. Let \(v_1,\dots,v_{|V|}\) be the processing order and \(A_i,B_i,C_i\) the classes when \(v_i\) is considered (only earlier vertices are in them).
  2. Per-vertex bound. Since \(v_i\) goes to the class minimizing \(w(v_i,\cdot)\), and the minimum of three numbers is at most their average, the illegal weight created is \[\min\big(w(v_i,A_i),w(v_i,B_i),w(v_i,C_i)\big)\le \tfrac13\big(w(v_i,A_i)+w(v_i,B_i)+w(v_i,C_i)\big).\]
