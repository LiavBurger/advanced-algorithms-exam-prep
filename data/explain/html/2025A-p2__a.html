The dual is \[(D)\quad \max\; c^Tz \quad\text{s.t.}\quad Az\le c,\ z\ge 0.\]

We first put \((P)\) in the standard equality form the dual recipe uses. Multiply \(Ax\ge c\) by \(-1\) to get \(-Ax\le -c\), then add slack \(s\ge 0\) to reach equalities:

\[ \min\; c^Tx \quad\text{s.t.}\quad -Ax+Is=-c,\ \ x,s\ge 0. \]

The recipe: for \(\min\{b^\top\xi: M\xi=d,\ \xi\ge 0\}\) the dual is \(\max\{d^\top y: M^\top y\le b,\ y\text{ free}\}\). Here \(\xi=(x,s)\), \(M=(-A\mid I)\), \(b=(c,0)\), and \(d=-c\). The two blocks of \(M^\top y\le b\) give \(-A^\top y\le c\) and \(Iy\le 0\), i.e. \(y\le 0\), with objective \(\max\,(-c)^\top y\). Since \(A^\top=A\), the first block is \(-Ay\le c\), so the dual is

\[ \max\; -c^Ty \quad\text{s.t.}\quad -Ay\le c,\ \ y\le 0. \]

Finally set \(z=-y\ge 0\); then \(-c^Ty=c^Tz\) and \(-Ay=Az\), giving the clean form \((D)\) above. Note \((D)\) is the mirror image of \((P)\): same \(A\), same \(c\), just \(\max\) with \(\le\) instead of \(\min\) with \(\ge\).

Intuition. Because \(A\) is symmetric the dual comes back looking almost exactly like the primal, so the LP is essentially self-dual.
