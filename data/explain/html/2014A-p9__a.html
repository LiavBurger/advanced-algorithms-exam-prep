Claim Any maximal matching \(M\) is a \(2\)-approximation for maximum matching: \(|M|\ge\tfrac12|N|\) where \(N\) is a maximum matching.

Strategy Show every edge of \(N\) is blocked by a vertex that \(M\) already uses, then count those vertices.

Key idea Maximality means no edge can be added, so every optimal edge must share an endpoint with some edge of \(M\).

Why this move "Maximal but maybe not maximum" invites comparing the two matchings vertex by vertex, and maximality is precisely a statement about which vertices are already occupied.

  1. Every \(N\)-edge is blocked. Take any \(e'\in N\). At least one endpoint of \(e'\) is used by some edge of \(M\); otherwise \(M\cup\{e'\}\) would still be a matching, contradicting maximality.
  2. Charge each \(N\)-edge to a used vertex. Map \(e'\) to one of its endpoints lying in \(V(M)\), the set of vertices \(M\) touches, and note \(|V(M)|\le 2|M|\).
  3. The map is injective. Edges of \(N\) are vertex-disjoint, so distinct \(N\)-edges map to distinct vertices.
  4. Count. The injection gives \(|N|\le|V(M)|\le 2|M|\), so \(|M|\ge\tfrac12|N|\).

Since \(M\) also cannot exceed \(N\), any maximal matching sits within a factor \(2\) of the best one, which is the claimed \(2\)-approximation.
